# One march clock and the game's own ways (Nov 18)

The job: every estimate of how fast a party moves and when it arrives (the rules inside the game, the reports, the war room's tools) goes through one march clock and one way-finder, and the way-finder becomes an exact copy of the game's own. No test runs until the whole job is done.

Why: in run 20261117-2146-A the storm guard counted my army at speed 2.8 while it marched about a map unit an hour through forest, Dol Guldur switched the guard off, and its storm of Imnagath ran into my arrival (module 0.20.5 fixed the guard's pace and added the storm's clock). An audit then found that most rules inside the game use a party's exact pace but on open ground over a straight line, that nothing adds a margin for an enemy out of sight, that the rules forget an enemy 6 hours after losing sight, and that our way-finder is close to the game's but not exact (on 69,495 comparisons with the ways the game planned: half within 1%, 9 in 10 within 5%, 1 in 100 off by 14% or more).

## Decisions

- **In view:** a party's exact pace from what is seen (the game's own speed sum), stretch by stretch for the ground and the hour. A party in a holding, a siege or a battle, whose stored speed the game stops updating, gets a fresh one from the game's own formula (`PartySpeedModel.CalculateBaseSpeed`, which may be called for any active party).
- **Out of sight up to 24 hours:** its last-seen pace +10%, stretch by stretch, from where it was seen, the clock running since the sighting.
- **Out of sight 24 hours to 3 days:** on each stretch the faster of its last-seen pace +10% and speed 2.8 (as measured, on any ground: it is the average of lord parties as they actually marched); the agent is told both.
- **Out of sight over 3 days:** not counted by the rules; still listed for the agent with where, when and at what speed.
- **Never seen:** not counted, as before.
- **An army out of sight:** the faster of the army's pace and its leader's own (the army's base scaled by its men: ((200 + army's men) / (200 + his men))^0.4; for my army at Imnagath 1.44 gives 2.02, the measured 2.02).
- **A party in a battle:** its march starts at the battle's expected end.
- **News without sight** (a battle or siege named where a lord is): a sighting there and then, keeping his last pace; taken prisoner removes him.
- **The agent's judgement:** an order setting to count a given force at its own last-seen pace instead of the minimum (default no).
- **Ways:** our own way-finder, made an exact copy of the game's (`MobileParty.ComputePath` → `GetPathBetweenAIFaces`: agent radius 0.3, the party's closed ground by face group, an extra cost multiplier of 2 on some faces, land and sea switch costs). It searches as far as it is asked: the walking mesh has 19,396 faces, so a search over the whole map takes milliseconds, on a side thread. A party already marching to the very place asked about keeps the way the game planned for it.
- **Until the copy passes its proof** the rules use a straight line with the ground read along it, marked "rough"; today's way-finder stays in the war room's tools, in reserve.
- **Left as they are:** the copy of the game's attack-or-flee choice (it mirrors the game's own numbers); the borders' "near your lands", a fixed measure at speed 2.8.

## Phase 2 notes: the game's own way-finder in TaleWorlds.Native.dll (1.4.8)

- The managed call parties use (`MapScene.GetPathBetweenAIFaces` with region switch costs, `MobileParty.ComputePath`) is the engine binding `IScene.get_path_between_ai_face_indices_with_region_switch_cost`, number 941 in `ManagedCallbacks.ScriptingInterfaceObjects.EngineInterfaceGeneratedEnum` (TaleWorlds.Engine.AutoGenerated.dll, decompiled with ilspycmd under DOTNET_ROLL_FORWARD=Major); `get_path_distance_between_ai_faces` is 944, `get_path_between_ai_face_indices` (no region costs) 940.
- The engine registers each binding with its code: at +0x3957e3, `lea r8,[rip+0x140756]; mov edx,0x3AD; call +0x3986e0`, so binding 941's code is at **+0x4D5F40** (the wrapper).
- The wrapper (+0x4D5F40): the scene's face table at **scene+0x3218** (an array of face pointers, indexed by face index: start and end faces); with excluded ground, a set is built (+0x41F2F0) and registered with the scene's walking mesh object at **scene+0x2DA0** (+0x415C30, returns a handle); with an agent radius above 0 it calls **+0x4E8080** (navmesh, start face, start and end points, radius, the path out, the exclusion handle, the extra cost multiplier, the two region switch costs); with radius 0 another routine.
- Tools: C:\BannerlordArchive	ools (Disasm, ImmScan, Callers, XRefs, Hunt, NativeStrings).
- **+0x4E8080** (radius above 0): calls the search **+0x4F81B0**, then walks the found path (32-byte entries in a small-buffer vector: inline when byte [v+0x1024] is set, count [v+0x1020]; else [v+0x1000]..[v+0x1008]) reading each entry's face (+8) and its next face (+0x10): face flag byte +0x139 bit 4, a pointer at +0x118, a byte +0xA8 (more than 3: the face's corner count?), four pointers at +0xB0 (edges or neighbours), floats +0xD8 and +0xE4. Likely the straightening, with special faces (flag 4) handled apart. To read next.
- **The search, +0x4F81B0** (scene's mesh object, start face, end face, start point, end point, radius, path out, ...): fails unless both faces share the island at face **+0x128**; asks **+0x414120** of the start face and, if that says no, tries a direct line (**+0x50F230**), which, when clear, gives a one-leg path. Otherwise A*: a search slot per thread (**+0x41E990** gives it; generation stamps at mesh **+0x5F8 + slot*4**, bumped by **+0x4178C0**); each face keeps its visit stamp at **face+0x40 + slot*4** and its parent at **face + slot*8**; the open list is a binary min-heap of 24-byte entries {face (8), g (4), f (4, compared), point (8)}, seeded with the start face, g 0, f the straight distance between the two points. A per-group byte table at mesh **+0x5A8**, indexed by the face's group at **face+0x130**, marks closed ground (from the exclusion handle). The expansion (neighbours, the crossing point, the radius test, the extra cost, the region costs) is next.
- **The expansion** (+0x4F8609 on): for the popped face, each of its edges (corner count byte **face+0xA8**; edge pointers **face+0xB0**[i]; the neighbour across edge i at **face+0x60**[i]), then extra links in a vector at **face+0xF8..+0x100** (with special faces, flag 4 at **+0x139**, linked through **+0x118**). A neighbour is skipped when: it is one-way (byte **+0x138**) and does not link back; its ability mask **+0x134** has none of the search's bits; its group **+0x130** is in the excluded list; its flags **+0x139** lack bit 1 or have bit 2; or it was reached already (stamp above the generation: **no re-opening, the first reach is final**).
- **The crossing point:** the edge from corner A (**[edge]**) to corner B (**[edge+8]**), corners as {x, y} floats with a flag byte at **+0x10**. When the mesh's radius switch is on (byte **[mesh+0x570]+0x48**), each corner whose flag byte has bit 0x80 or bit (1 << layer) (the corner touches the mesh's edge) moves along the edge toward the other by min(k x the edge's length, the radius), k = **0.49** when both corners are so flagged, **0.99** when one is (rva 0xB2B394, 0xB2B514): the portal never closes. The crossing point is the point of that portal nearest the current entry's point (the projection, clamped to the portal).
- **The step's cost:** g' = g + |current point - crossing point| + (extra cost of the face left + of the face entered, floats at **face+0x120**) x **0.5** (rva 0xB2B39C) x the extra cost multiplier (2 for a party's own ways, 1 for most other callers) + the region switch cost when the two faces' regions differ (the group's byte in the table at mesh+0x5A8: land or sea; the cost to the region entered). f' = g' + |crossing point - end point| + the switch cost when the face entered is not in the end's region. The heap holds {face, g', f', crossing point}; the face's parent is the face left; it is marked open (generation + 1), and closed (generation + 2) when popped. Done when the end face is closed; the path is then built by **+0x417970** (slot, start face, end face, end point, start point, path out), to read next with +0x4E8080's pass over it.
- **The first proof, read** (Nov 19, the 200 logged differences, scratchpad tool NavProbe): the game's bends sit exactly 0.30 from a mesh corner along one of its edges, so the portal shortening is right; the corners the engine treats as closed are those with flag 0x80 or the bit it gave the party's set of closed ground (bit 1 here: 15,708 corners, all 15,638 corners of closed faces among them), now used directly (copy version 2), which changed no way. In 164 of the 182 differences where the game has fewer corners, every corner of the game's way is one of the copy's, in order: the copy bends at extra points. Through a fan of faces round one corner the game bends once, 0.3 off the corner, and passes the fan's later portals about 0.27 from it, inside the copy's shortened portals; using one point per corner for the whole fan made things worse (median length gap 0.13%). In some differences the game's way also runs outside one of the copy's portals, so the corridor differs there. Not yet matched: the engine's straightening in +0x417970 after the corridor (constants at rva 0xB2B008 and on) and its pass over the path in +0x4E8080 after the search (calls +0x416140 thirteen times, +0x4F8BC0 twice, +0x4F2730 at the end). Simple line-of-sight smoothing after the funnel matched 5 to 7 of the 200.
- **The doorways, +0x417970** (slot, start face, end face, end point, start point, path out): no straightening here. Walking back from the end face by the search's parents, one 32-byte entry per face but the start's, each entry {point, face, edge, flags} with the edge between corridor faces k and k + 1 and the face k (the start side); the last entry is the end point in the end face, marked a bend (flag 0x8000 in the word at +0x18). A start or end point outside its face gets one more entry, where the line from the face's centre crosses its edge (not needed for points inside).
- **The pass, +0x4E8080** (after the search): splits the entries at fixed points (an entry whose edge has a face flagged 8 on either side gets its edge's middle; an end in such a face gets the face's centre; special faces, flag 4, have their own rules, none on this map), runs the straightening **+0x4F8BC0** on each stretch from the last fixed point, then keeps only the entries marked as bends (+0x4F2730 cuts the list). The way handed back is their points: the start left out, the end last.
- **The straightening, +0x4F8BC0**, three passes over a stretch. (1) A funnel through the doorways, apex-relative: each closed corner (flag 0x80 or the party's bit) moved toward the other by min(0.4 x the edge's length, the radius) whichever ends are closed (not the search's 0.49 and 0.99); a doorway touching the apex exactly is skipped; left and right by the sign of the cross product; first the test whether the right side crossed the left, then the reverse, then each side narrowed; a bend sets that entry's point to the corner and marks it. (2) Bends dropped by line of sight: from an anchor (the start, then kept bends), each bend the straight line from the anchor to the next bend reaches is dropped (the anchor's face is the stretch's first entry's for the start), and after each drop the line is tried from up to ten entries further back, dropping the anchor while it reaches. (3) A region change (land and sea) between bends gets a bend where the line between them crosses its doorway. The line test is **+0x50F320**: the end in the first face or the faces one, open; otherwise a walk **+0x51C3F0** through every edge whose portal (narrowed as in the funnel) the line crosses on its way out of a face (the face's inward normal against the direction, within 0.0001), into faces the question may enter, with no extra cost and no costly region switch, each face visited once a line (a fresh generation per line; the search's marks are three generations older); open on reaching the end face with the end inside it (within 0.0025 as twice the area, +0x416370), or leaving the end face by any portal. The search's own straight line before it searches (+0x50F230, walk +0x51DCC0) follows the same rules and records the faces. Segments meet within 0.001 of their lengths (+0x41F970). The engine's 1/sqrt is rsqrtss with one Newton step; the copy starts from the exact value, a difference in the last bit.
- **What it explains:** the game's way leaves the searched corridor wherever the straight line through other faces is open, so it bends less than a funnel through the corridor alone, and passes a fan's later doorways closer to the corner than the corridor's narrowed doorways allow. The edges of this map's faces are listed counter-clockwise, edge i from corner i to corner i + 1 (all 19,396 faces); an edge between two faces is kind 2, one on the mesh's edge kind 1.
- **Done** (Nov 20): the in-game proof passed, 2,000 of 2,000. A new map (a different walking mesh) copies its mesh and proves the copy again at its first load; until then the straight line. Left: Phase 1's replay of recorded games before and after, now with the game's own ways.
