# Engine notes: Bannerlord 1.4.8 + TAOM 2.0.26

What the game and the TAOM pack do, as read in their code and seen in play while building the agents. Verified on my laptop (the Legion); the decompiled source and the tools named here live on it, outside this repository.

- Decompiled 1.4.8 source: `C:\BannerlordArchive\refsrc\TW148` (laptop only). `C:\BannerlordArchive\tools\RefCheck` lists every game member a DLL references that no longer exists.
- Movement commands are on `MobileParty`, not `MobilePartyAi`: `SetMoveGoToSettlement(s, NavigationType, isTargetingPort)`, `SetMoveGoToPoint(CampaignVec2, NavigationType)`, `SetMoveEngageParty`, `SetMoveGoAroundParty`, `SetMoveEscortParty`, `SetMoveBesiegeSettlement`, `SetMoveRaidSettlement`, `SetMoveDefendSettlement`, `SetMovePatrolAroundSettlement` / `SetMovePatrolAroundPoint`, `SetMoveModeHold`. Stop the stock AI for a party with `party.Ai.SetDoNotMakeNewDecisions(true)`. `SetInitiative(attack, avoid, hours)` also exists.
- Visibility is computed only for the player (`PartyBase.UpdateVisibilityAndInspected`). Faction views are ours to compute: each observer's `MobileParty.SeeingRange` against `Campaign.Current.Models.MapVisibilityModel.GetPartySpottingRatioForMainPartySeeingRange(target)`.
- Diplomacy: `DeclareWarAction`, `MakePeaceAction`. Alliances and calls to war are kingdom decisions (`StartAllianceDecision`, `ProposeCallToWarAgreementDecision`, `AcceptCallToWarAgreementDecision`) backed by `AllianceCampaignBehavior` / `AllianceModel`.
- Clock: `Campaign.Current.TimeControlMode` and `Campaign.Current.SetTimeControlModeLock(bool)` (not used for agents: the clock never stops).
- A frozen party still takes the initiative against an enemy that is in a map event at, or besieging, its target settlement (`MobilePartyAi`, around line 486). This is expected behaviour, not disobedience.
- Events used: `HourlyTickEvent`, `TickEvent`, `OnBeforeSaveEvent`, `OnSaveOverEvent(bool, string)`, `OnGameLoadFinishedEvent`, `MapEventStarted(MapEvent, PartyBase, PartyBase)`, `OnSiegeEventStartedEvent(SiegeEvent)`, `ArmyCreated(Army)`.
- Player visibility: `PartyBase.UpdateVisibilityAndInspected` → `CalculateVisibilityAndInspected` compares the distance against `MapVisibilityModel.GetPartySpottingRatioForMainPartySeeingRange(target)`. The faction-view rule must reproduce this exactly for the main party; that is the recorder's vision check.
- Party AI Controls (installed 1.4.5; decompile of the same names at `C:\BannerlordArchive\refsrc\PAI`) holds vassal and clan-party orders. The static `PartyAIControls.SubModule.PartySettingsManager` is a `PartyAIClanPartySettingsManager`. A lord's current order is `settings.Order`: a `PartyAIControls.PAICustomOrder` with a target and a nested `OrderType` (DefendSettlement, PatrolAroundPoint, PatrolClanLands, StayInSettlement, RecruitFromTemplate) plus `Text`. **Never call `Settings(Hero)` from this project except in `PacLink`:** for a lord without an entry it ADDS one to `_partySettings` (PartyAIClanPartySettingsManager.cs ~371–404), and `_partySettings` is saved with the game (`SyncData`, ~566). Read the internal `HeroesWithOrders` list (~207) or the `_partySettings` dictionary instead. `PacLink` (0.11.1, the lieutenant) does make entries, for the ruler's realm's lords only: the ones made just for a mirror, the mirrors themselves and the vassals put on TFAOMFixes' `VassalOrders.Commanded` for them are taken out before every save and put back after; what `party_ai` sets stays, as Party AI Controls' own state. The decompile at refsrc\PAI is OLDER than the installed 1.4.5 (no `RecruitFromEnemySettlements`, no `SetPartyTemplate`, `DismissUnwantedTroops` default true there, false installed): decompile the installed `Modules\PartyAIControls\bin\Win64_Shipping_Client\PartyAIUpdated.dll` (`ilspycmd -p`) before relying on a member.
- Hardware: Core Ultra 9 275HX = 8 P-cores + 16 E-cores (24 threads); RTX 5090 Laptop, 24 GB. In big battles the main thread runs at ~90% of a core, the process uses ~15% of all cores, and the GPU is 21–41% busy. The campaign map has not been measured yet. A TAOM 2.0.26 campaign has about 3,350 parties.
- Harmony, if needed: `new Harmony(id).PatchAll(typeof(SubModule).Assembly)`. A bare `PatchAll()` throws under BLSE and hides the crash window, which looks like a black-screen boot stall. Use the Harmony 2.4.2 that TAOM.Dependencies bundles.
- TAOM.Dependencies' PatchShield silently REMOVES every foreign patch on a method once any patch on it throws MissingMethod, MissingField or TypeLoad. Log which patches are really in place at session launch.
- Patches on tiny getters get bypassed because callers inline them: patch the caller. TAOM wraps many game models: patch the runtime model's public method (`Campaign.Current.Models.X.GetType()`), not a default model's private worker. Never scale a saved value inside a getter, because it compounds every session.
- Logs to read: `C:\ProgramData\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\logs\rgl_log_<pid>.txt` (engine) and the TAOM crash bundles in `<game>\bin\Win64_Shipping_Client\logs\taom_crash_*.zip` (read report.txt first).
- Any change to the enabled module list costs one slow load while the shaders rebuild.
- **When things tick (1.4.8, verified Oct 8).** `Campaign.Tick` fires `CampaignEvents.TickEvent` and the periodic (hourly) events only while `_dt > 0`: not while paused, not in a town menu unless waiting, and not in a battle (MapState does not tick then). So `Background.ToMain` (agent orders) waits for the clock; the agent gets a RECEIVED receipt meanwhile. `MBSubModuleBase.OnApplicationTick` runs every frame in every state (the heartbeat).
- **A second thread touches parties.** `MapScreen.OnPostFrameTick` invokes `Campaign.CampaignLateAITickTask` (`Campaign.LateAITick` → `PartiesThink` → every party's `MobilePartyAi.Tick`) as an async task; `Campaign.RealTick` waits for it at the start of the next frame. So campaign event handlers are safe, but `OnApplicationTick` can overlap it: read no party state there. Hooks on movement code may run on that thread.
- **Every movement command** (`MobileParty.SetMove*`) starts with the private `MobileParty.ResetAllMovementParameters()` (~55 bytes of IL, not inlined): the one door for watching plan changes.
- **Armies and held lords (read in the 1.4.8 source for 0.4; not yet all seen in a game):** a party with `DoNotMakeNewDecisions` also loses its quick reactions (no fleeing), so every tool carries our own safety rule. Besiegers assault by themselves once their siege engines are ready. `Kingdom.CreateArmy(leader, gatheringPoint, type, members)` charges the clan influence per member in `Army.OnAddPartyInternal` (price from `ArmyManagementCalculationModel.CalculatePartyInfluenceCost`); members march to the leader through the AI's escort behaviour; the army disbands after about 48 idle hours, on cohesion 0, when over half its parties starve, or with no war. A mercenary clan's lord cannot lead; the ruler cannot be called as a member. Setting `army.AiBehaviorObject` keeps the gathering point the agent named; `CreateArmy` fires `ArmyCreated` before that, so the notice takes the place from `Orders.FormingAt`. Seen in the game (Oct 9): armies form at once and called lords follow (EscortParty). A tool's first step judges the world from the hour's copy, taken before its own order acted: anything the order itself changes (an army) is judged from the next hour (gather's false "broke up before it formed", 0.5.0).
- **The game's own history (`Campaign.Current.LogEntryHistory.GameActionLogs`, read for 0.5):** entries are deleted after `KeepInHistoryTime`, 7 days by default (a peace 40 days, a capture 84, alliances and calls to war 4 years, a death never). `IEncyclopediaLog.GetEncyclopediaText()` is the game's sentence with encyclopedia links (`<a ...><b>Name</b></a>`): strip the tags. `ChangeSettlementOwnerLogEntry.IsVisibleInEncyclopediaPageOf` is false for a change by siege (`SiegeAftermathLogEntry` covers it), so read its `PreviousClan`/`NewClan` fields instead. New events used: `OnSiegeEventEndedEvent`, `HeroKilledEvent`, `HeroPrisonerReleased` (with `EndCaptivityDetail`, namespace `TaleWorlds.CampaignSystem.Actions`).
- **Sieges and relief (read in 1.4.8 for 0.6):** a party attacking a besieger starts a `SiegeOutside` battle (`StartBattleAction.Apply`; `MapEvent.Initialize` leaves `MapEventSettlement` null there, so `AddInsideSettlementParties` does not run); all besieging parties join the defending side. The garrison and the lords inside join only through `SallyOutsCampaignBehavior`: on `MapEventStarted` and every settlement hour, when (inside strength for `SallyOut` + friendly parties near the camp, aggressiveness-weighted) > 1.5 x (enemy parties near the camp), they are put on the relief's side (the event becomes `SallyOut`). Without a relief the bar is 2x, checked when the hour is divisible by 4. `Town.GetDefenderParties(SallyOut)` leaves militia out. TAOM does not touch any of it.
- **Who leads a battle's side, and what that does (read in 1.4.8, Nov 25, module 0.24.7 / war room 0.20.10):** each side's advantage is its leader party's alone: 1 + 0.001 x the leader hero's Tactics (`DefaultCombatSimulationModel.GetBattleAdvantage`, `DefaultSkillEffects.TacticsAdvantage`), minus 0.1 for the attackers only while the battle is a storm (`BattleTypes.Siege`). A side led by a settlement (a storm's defenders) has no leader hero, so its advantage is 1. A relief that joins a storm turns it into SiegeOutside (`MapEvent.AddInvolvedParty`) but does not change the defenders' leader: the lords who came fight under the castle, at advantage 1, and the -0.1 is gone from the besiegers. In battle every troop's power takes its side leader's captain bonus (`Hero.PowerModifier`, cached; `DefaultMilitaryPowerModel.GetTroopPower`), in place of its own lord's: a party's estimated strength counts its own lord's, a castle-led side counts none. A struck side's morale above 50 weakens each blow it takes by up to 25% at 100 (`CalculateSimulationMoraleEffects`). The defenders' morale floor of 30 holds only in a storm (`MapEventSide`); a settlement-led side never breaks (`MapEvent`); only a field battle has retreats (`DefaultEncounterModel`), a battle outside the walls has none. TAOM's combat and power models add no race or culture factor to the simulation (a refuge damage cut, tier base powers, and culture survival odds that only decide wounded against killed). Run 20261125-1457-C2's battle at Lasgalor (863 men growing to 15,900 over a day, all under `castle_M4` at advantage 1.00, morale about 99.5, against Lorgath's 13,579 at 1.33 and morale 100): the hourly frames run through GameBattle with each hour's recorded numbers come within 15 to 20% of the real losses on both sides (the rules run about 1.18 times slow on both, likely the random pairing of strikers and targets in `MapEvent`), and the ratio between the sides comes out right. The forecasts that said the kingdom would win had the inputs wrong, not the rules.
- **The player clan gets no daily clan upkeep (read in 1.4.8, Nov 25, module 0.24.6):** `ClanVariablesCampaignBehavior.DailyTickClan` runs, for every clan but `Clan.PlayerClan`, `MakeClanFinancialEvaluation` (each war party's wage payment limit from the leader's gold: the full limit above 90,000, 600 to 1,200 from 30,000, 200 to 600 below, half again for the leader's own party), `UpdateGovernorsOfClan`, `UpdateClanSettlementsPaymentLimit` and `UpdateClanSettlementAutoRecruitment`. The player clan is a fixed object (`Campaign.PlayerDefaultFaction`, the clan with id `player_faction`, found at each load); switching the player character (`ChangePlayerCharacterAction`) does not change it. In a playerless run the stand-in is a companion of that clan and the ruler leads it, so House of Telcontar ruled the kingdom without that upkeep: its six parties kept their full wage limits and its gold was gone by day 27 of run 20261125-0020-C2 (16,412 a day in wages on day 1). Since 0.24.6 `RulingClanUpkeep` runs those four steps for it each day (by name), only while the player's hero is not its leader; the agent's wage caps go back on right after (`Wages.OnDailyClan`).
- **Why the game's own armies gave up their storms after a reload (found Nov 25, module 0.24.5):** the game keeps each realm's enemies in a cached list (`IFaction.FactionsAtWarWith`), rebuilt when a save loads (`CampaignFactionManagerBehaviour.OnGameLoaded`, from `IsAtWarWith`) and at each war, peace or call to war (`FactionManager`). The war planner (`AiMilitaryBehavior.FindBestTargetAndItsValueForFaction`) looks for siege and raid targets only among that list's realms. With TAOM, a war can come from its diplomacy model (`TaomDiplomacyModel.IsAtConstantWar`: the War of the Ring blocks peace between hostile tiers), and when the game rebuilds the lists that answer is not yet there for the player-founded realm (`new_kingdom`): measured at a load of a mid-storm save, seven realms' lists lacked the Kingdom of Arnor and Gondor (Dol Guldur, Isengard, empire, empire_s, khuzait, aserai, bluecraig) and its own list lacked all seven, while `IsAtWarWith` said war. So an army of those realms storming or besieging a kingdom place when the game saved was offered no military option at its first decision after the load (Dol Guldur: 38 enemy forts before the save, 18 after, none near), its leader chose a visit, and `AiPartyThinkBehavior.PartyHourlyAiTick` ends the storm and the siege when a camp's leader picks anything else. The lists stayed short until the next war or peace touching that realm rebuilt them (the 1.5 to 2 days seen). It also blinded the kingdom's own game-led lords to its enemies after every load (the C0 control). Other realms' storms (Shaghana at castle_A1, empire_w) carried on: their wars are plain stances. Found with `AiProbe` (each storming leader's gates at its decisions) and `autopilot.ps1 -StormRepro`. Fix: `WarLists` rebuilds every list by the game's own method at `OnGameLoadFinished` (the map state's, after every mod's session start) and checks the kingdoms' lists daily; the replayed save's storm then went on through the watch, the leader's first decision again 16 military options.
- **Where the game shows a besieged place's days of food (read in 1.4.8, Nov 24, module 0.24.4):** only the siege screen (`EncounterMenuOverlayVM`): `UpdateProperties` sets the defenders' `DefenderPartyFood` ("N days of food left") from the town's food and market stocks over `FoodChangeWithoutMarketStocks`, and `GetEncounterSideFoodTooltip` lists "Settlement's Days Until Food Runs Out" the same way; both sides' players see it. The town food tooltip (`CampaignUIHelper.GetTownFoodTooltip`) shows the stock and its change, no days. Since 0.24.4 (`SiegeFoodScreen`, two postfixes found by name, only with siege_food on, the place besieged and lords inside carrying food) both count the lords' food as the siege food rule does (`SiegeFoodRules.Days`), and the tooltip adds "Food Carried by the Lords Inside".
- **A relief never fights behind the walls (read in 1.4.8, Nov 24, module 0.24.3):** a party attacking a besieger starts a `SiegeOutside` battle against the whole camp (`StartBattleAction.Apply`), and the combat model weighs the walls only for `BattleTypes.Siege` (`DefaultCombatSimulationModel.GetSimulationTicksForBattleRound`; `GetSimulationTickInterval` gives a storm 60 minutes a round, everything else 30). When a storm is being fought and a party that is not inside the settlement joins the defenders, `MapEvent` (where it adds an involved party: `IsSiegeAssault && party.MobileParty.CurrentSettlement == null && side == Defender`) turns the storm itself into `SiegeOutside`: from then the garrison and the relief fight the whole camp in the open, two rounds an hour, no walls. Run 20261124-1400-C2: Gaín's army (7,600 strength) joined the Nazgûl's storm of Goblin Town; the game recorded a 35-hour `SiegeOutside` battle, Gaín's army and the garrison (6,909 men, 18 lords) all fell, and a new storm took the town minutes after. `GameBattle.Run` does the same since 0.24.3 (`BattleOutcome.OutsideAt`).
- **Where volunteers are (read Oct 29, 0.13.3):** the base game gives notables to towns (2 merchants, 2 gang leaders, 1 artisan) and villages (1 headman, 2 rural notables) only (`DefaultNotableSpawnModel`); TAOM keeps those counts, some peoples' feats changing them (`TaomNotableSpawnModel`), and adds castle recruiting (`TAOM.Features.CastleRecruitment`: 3 notables a castle by default, gang leader, headman, merchant; its AI switch on by default, so the game's lords recruit there too). Each notable holds 6 volunteers (`Hero.VolunteerTypes`). In our records castles gave the most men in all (147k of 268k), towns the most a visit (about 10 against 6). `RecruitTour.MayRecruitHere` takes any place with notables; since 0.13.4 `Recruitment`'s day log counts castles too (volunteers waiting, and hours where a lord could recruit), and defend takes its own castle's volunteers before riding to a town or village.
- **Recruiting (1.4.8, `RecruitmentCampaignBehavior`):** lords the game runs recruit in `HourlyTickParty` only while at a town (not besieged) or unraided village, held lords included (it does not check `DoNotMakeNewDecisions`); gates: `PartyTradeGold > HeroHelper.StartRecruitingMoneyLimit` (50 + 20 x min(150, men)), non-leaders also `Clan.Gold > StartRecruitingMoneyLimitForClanLeader` (50 + leader party wages + 40 x leader party men), room below the size limit, not starving, `GetAvailableWageBudget()` per recruit, notable slots below `VolunteerModel.MaximumIndexHeroCanRecruitFromHero`. `Clan.Gold` is the leader's gold.
- **Patrol and held lords (1.4.8, read for 0.6.1):** the game's patrol (`PatrolAroundPoint`, `GetLandPatrolBehavior`) walks to random reachable points in a ring between 0.5r and r from the gate, r = `FortificationPatrolDistanceAsDays` (0.5) or `VillagePatrolDistanceAsDays` (0.25) x 24 x the party's own speed: 20 to 41 map units from a castle at the orcs' median speed of 3.4. TAOM does not change it. So tools with a `radius` (defend, screen) patrol with our own `SetMoveGoToPoint` points instead (`Orders.NextPatrolPoint`). A held party (`DoNotMakeNewDecisions`) skips the initiative step (no chase, no flight) except against an enemy in a battle at, or besieging, its own `TargetSettlement` (`MobilePartyAi.GetBehaviors`, line 486). An AI party sent into a besieged friendly town or castle simply enters at the gate (`EncounterManager.StartSettlementEncounter`), and once inside it cannot be called out (it is part of the defence).
- **Would a place hold (1.4.8, read for 0.6.2):** besiegers assault only when `StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical` (BesiegerCamp): besieger strength > defenders' `CalculateCurrentStrength` (every party inside: garrison, militia, lords) x advantage^k, advantage = `GetSettlementAdvantage` = (1 + 4 + (wall level - 1)) / (1 + engine bonuses: +0.25 ram or tower, +0.16 ram or +0.24 improved ram, +0.16 one tower or +0.24 two, +0.08 per trebuchet/onager/ballista, +0.12 per fire engine), a quarter of the walls' part once they are breached; k = 0.8 (x1.25 without a ram, x1.25 without a tower), falling 0.02 an hour after 4 days. `World.WallFactor` takes a ram and two towers built (2.4 / 2.8 / 3.2 for wall levels 1 / 2 / 3). Wall level: `Town.GetWallLevel()` (fortifications building level). Militia: `Settlement.MilitiaPartyComponent.MobileParty`. A party in a settlement leaves by itself once its short-term target is elsewhere (`CheckExitingSettlementParallel`), unless it holds. Above the bar the assault is a roll each check (ratio x engines built/3 x a progress factor x 0.1), so stronger besiegers storm sooner; k reaches 0 about 136 h into a siege and goes negative after, so a long siege storms even below the defenders' bare strength. A player leading the siege is not bound by this test.
- **Party AI Controls wakes held lords (read for 0.6.3):** `PartyAIThinker.OnHourlyTickParty` (~150 in the decompile) switches `DoNotMakeNewDecisions` back off for every lord party it does not manage (`IsHeroManageable` false: not the player's clan, nor the player's kingdom's when it manages those) whose `DefaultBehavior == Hold` (0 in 1.4.8's enum: Hold, None, GoToSettlement, ...). Every held lord standing still (a gather leader waiting, a lord arrived at a village) was released this way each hour, and the game's hourly decision (`AiPartyThinkBehavior.PartyHourlyAiTick` runs only while the flag is off) moved it before our hourly re-issue. The game itself puts parties into Hold in ~36 places (battle ends, settlement entry, armies), so avoiding Hold in our own moves is not enough: `GameHooks.KeepHeldPatch` (prefix/postfix on that method, first argument by position) holds again a lord one of our orders holds. Nothing in the base game turns the flag off for a lord (only quests' own parties and `TeleportHeroAction`); Improved Garrisons only its own parties. Self-check "held lords kept held" fails when Party AI Controls is loaded and the hook did not attach.
- **Found after run C1 (Oct 23, 0.11.3):**
  - **Villages:** a lord the game runs steps into a village and straight back out, left standing at it (`EncounterManager.StartSettlementEncounter`'s last step: `LeaveSettlementAction` + `SetMoveModeHold`), so `CurrentSettlement` is null there. Use the game's own test, `MobilePartyHelper.GetCurrentSettlementOfMobilePartyForAICalculation` (its `LastVisitedSettlement` within 1), as `Orders.Where`/`At` do. Before, every village stop looked like "not arrived", and recruit trips bought at 2 of 1,562 village stops.
  - **A battle's end:** `MapEventEnded` fires after `CaptureDefeatedPartyMembers` has taken the losing lords from their parties (`LeaderHero` null; the parties stay in the side's list until `HandleMapEventEnd`). Count a side's lords at `MapEventStarted`, or from `MapEventParty.Troops`/`WoundedInBattle`/`DiedInBattle`.
  - **The player's clan is fixed:** `Clan.PlayerClan` is the clan `player_faction` (`Campaign.PlayerDefaultFaction`), whoever the player plays. With it ruling, a due vote waits for the player (`KingdomDecision.NeedsPlayerResolution`), and the map opens the kingdom screen for it only when no menu is up. `Votes` settles them as for a computer-run realm (`KingdomElection.StartElectionWithoutPlayer`) while the player is not the ruler. The game puts one holding's vote at a time, and a taken holding goes to the ruler's clan until then.
  - **TFAOMFixes' recruiting trip** (`LordRecruiting`): a computer lord below 50% ranks, not in the player's clan nor managed by Party AI Controls, is steered with the game's AI off to 80%. It waits while another switched the AI off, and takes him the hour after our order ends. It drops him only when our order's place differs from its own: `LordTours.Drop` takes him off it when any order of ours starts.
  - **Prisoners handed over** (`SellPrisonersAction`, what a computer lord does on entering a friendly castle or town): soldiers are sold. An enemy lord handed to one's own realm's holding goes to its dungeon as a gift that earns the realm influence (`InfluenceGainCampaignBehavior`), and then the game pays for none of that hand-over, its soldiers included; they are taken, not kept. That is the game's rule for every lord (Oct 23: lords can sometimes place prisoners in the dungeon instead of selling them); the record names it "prisoners given to a dungeon" (C1 had 27, all this rule). A castle pays for soldiers as a town does: the ransom is the man's, by the seller (`PrisonerRansomValue(character, sellerHero)`), and no ransom broker is involved for computer lords (only the player needs a town's). The influence is small: ransom^0.4 x 0.2 per prisoner given (`DefaultPrisonerDonationModel`), about 6 for a lord worth 5,000. The one-batch rule cost the kingdom about 6,300 prisoners in run 20261127-1453-C2 (60 of 305 hand-overs, towns 41, castles 19), so since 0.25.6 a lord holding one of our orders hands over in two batches through the same action, soldiers first, then the lords (`Purse.HandOver`, `PrisonerSalePatch`); the game's own lords keep the one batch.
  - **Hired clans** leaving a realm take their gold out of a realm's sum (five left the orcs in C1, 8.9M): the scoreboard keeps `hired_gold`.
- **Found in run C2 (Oct 23, 0.11.4):**
  - **The game's own sally** (`SallyOutsCampaignBehavior.CheckSallyOut(Settlement settlement, bool checkForNavalSallyOut, out bool salliedOut)`, private): every 4th in-game hour (`floor(hours) % 4 == 0`, from the settlement's hourly tick), and beside a relief's battle at the camp. Everyone inside (`GetInvolvedPartiesForEventType(SallyOut)`, strength as attacker in a plain battle) plus friendly parties within `GetEncounterJoiningRadius` (3) of the camp's leader go out when more than 2 times the enemy parties there not in a settlement, weighted by `Aggressiveness` (full above 0.5). Beside a relief: 1.5 times, and they join its battle. It starts the battle with the garrison as leader (`EncounterManager.StartPartyEncounter(GarrisonParty, leader)`), and the whole besieging army fights it in the open. Skipped when the player leads the defence from inside. No installed mod touches it. In C2 it sent Aragorn's and Imák's sheltering armies (5,938 men, 20 lords) out against Lorgath's 22.6k the hour his siege began: all lost. By our numbers it needed under 8k of Lorgath counted; why it counted so little is not known yet. 0.11.4's `SallyGate` keeps the defenders inside while one of our ordered lords is there, and records the game's numbers ("sally" events).
  - **At war:** `FactionManager.IsAtWarAgainstFaction` asks the diplomacy model first (TAOM's War of the Ring table, which TFAOMFixes `RingWars` fills for the player's realm), then the stance. `StanceLink.IsAtWar` alone says peace for the shadow's realms the player's realm was never declared on (Dol Guldur). TFAOMFixes fills the table at session start just after this mod, so check wars from the first hour, not at the load.
  - **A channel's mode:** `AgentChannel.ModeText()` (static) is the first agent's (`Primary`); a channel's own is its instance `ModeText()`. 0.11.3 and before headed every agent's poke and report with the first agent's mode, so in C2 the kingdom's live agent read "SHADOW ... none is carried out" (B too, where the orcs, listed first, are in shadow).
- **Army counts:** `Army.TotalHealthyMembers` and `EstimatedStrength` count the leader and ATTACHED parties only; a called lord still marching counts on its own. `MaximumDistanceToCallToArmy` = average distance between neighbouring towns x 8 (a cached lookup); the influence price rises with distance. `Army.Gather` with no members is fine (an army of its leader alone); inactivity (48 h) counts Hold, PatrolAroundPoint and GoToSettlement to a friendly place, not GoToPoint.
- **The AI's decision step:** `AiPartyThinkBehavior.PartyHourlyAiTick(MobileParty mobileParty)`, a listener of `TickPartialHourlyAiEvent` (tickers built with doParallel false: main thread). It returns at once if `Ai.DoNotMakeNewDecisions`; thinks every 1, 3 or 6 hours (`Ai.HourCounter`, reset to 0 then incremented, so 1 after a think); resets `mobileParty.ThinkParamsCache`, fires `AiHourlyTickEvent` for the behaviours to add `(AIBehaviorData, score)` pairs, picks the best; moves to it only if `MBRandom.RandomFloat < best` (up to N rolls) and best > a bar (0.03 for patrol/visit, else 0.1; x2 when gathering an army, x0.33 for an army leader), or always when holding with a score over 0.01. Gathering an army calls `Kingdom.CreateArmy`.
- **Ways and the walking map (read in 1.4.8 for 0.7, docs/ROUTES.md):** `MobileParty.ComputePath` asks `MapSceneWrapper.GetPathBetweenAIFaces(CurrentNavigationFace, target.Face, ..., agentRadius 0.3, Path, invalid face groups, extraCostMultiplier 2f, region switch costs)`: the shortest way over walkable faces (what the multiplier applies to is in the engine; the dashboard's accuracy check weighs three guesses). `Path` is a public `NavigationPath` (128 corners, `Size`); the party walks `Path[PathBegin]` onward while the private `_pathMode` is on (`DoAiPathMode`), else straight at its target; it plans again when its target moves. The cached final speed is the internal `_lastCalculatedSpeed`; `LastCalculatedBaseSpeed` is the base. Walkable for land (`DefaultPartyNavigationModel`): plain, desert, snow, forest, steppe, swamp, dune, bridge, fording, beach (farmland `RuralArea` is not); no naval in the default model. Speed (`DefaultPartySpeedCalculatingModel.CalculateFinalSpeed`): forest −30%, water/river/bridge/ford −30%, desert/dune −10%, snowfall −10%, night −25% (night = outside `CampaignTime.SunRise`..`SunSet`, 2..22 by default). The face group index is the `TerrainType`. - **Road distances, watch and shadow (0.7.1, 2026-10-16):** every distance the agent reads is given as the crow flies and by road ("63.0 (260.0 by road)"), the road figure being the game's own: its navigation cache holds the real way between every two holdings' gates (filled for all pairs at campaign start or read from the pack's `ModuleData/DistanceCaches/settlements_distance_cache_Default.bin`, so `MapDistanceModel.GetDistance(Settlement, Settlement, ...)` is a lookup; a pair missing would be searched and cached by the game), copied at load into `RoadBook` (`Capture.RoadsFor`: the two followed realms' holdings to all, each holding to its 12 nearest); from a party, through `GetClosestEntranceToFace(CurrentNavigationFace)` (copied hourly as `PartyRow.Entrance`), as `DefaultMapDistanceModel.GetDistance(MobileParty, Settlement)` does. The game's own party-to-party distance is only the straight line; ours goes entrance to entrance. Spots (`p673_994`) name points on the map for the `watch` order (`post` move); `shadow` re-works its point every hour. A rule's `set` runs before its call-back text is filled: a call-back must not name a value its own rule clears.
- **Siege timing and army pace (read in 1.4.8 for 0.7.2, after Hollin Gate; docs/TOOLS.md):** an AI besieger storms only via `MobilePartyAi.GetBesiegeBehavior` when `BesiegerCamp.IsReadyToBesiege`: `IsPreparationComplete` (the `preparations` engine, 48 man-days in Native's `siegeengines.xml`, built at `GetConstructionProgressPerHour` = √(healthy men) man-days a day: ~26 h for 2,000 men), then at least one engine built (`GetNumberOfEquipmentsBuilt` > 0, else the score is multiplied by 0; ram 12 man-days, ballista 8; engines start only after preparations, `DefaultSiegeStrategyActionModel` line ~151), then a random roll each check (`StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical`). The siege camp's work runs in `SiegeEvent`, untouched by `DoNotMakeNewDecisions`. A party's land pace is `4 × (200/(200+men))^0.4` with ALL attached parties' men, wounded, prisoners, herds and cargo pooled on the army leader (`CalculateLandBaseSpeed`); the attached parties' own speeds in our rows mean nothing. The besieged realm's AI sends free lords to the holding one by one (DefendSettlement), and each joins a storm already under way: the relief alarm counts lords, not armies (`World.ReliefComing`). The game clock never stops while the agent thinks (PLAN.md): about 1 in-game hour per 3.5 real seconds at the speed I play.
- **The game's clocks (0.7.3, 2026-10-17, docs/TOOLS.md "The game's clocks"):** a party covers **speed / 1.2 map units an in-game hour** (`Campaign.Tick`: 4,320 s of map time per dt; measured 0.81 on Oct 16's frames); the speed is base x (1 + one sum of factors), the ground and night ADDED into it (not multiplied), at least 1; the active speed model is TAOM's `TaomPartySpeedModel` (vanilla final speed + peoples' ground/night feats + hero career passives). Read the base parts from `MobileParty._lastCalculatedBaseSpeedExplained` (the game's own cache, no new sum) and measure the rest from `_lastCalculatedSpeed` each hour (`GamePace.Residual`; on Oct 16 about +0.19 per lord, likely Uncanny Insight from boosted Scouting): never multiply 0.7/0.75 factors again. Several mods adding one model: the last registered wins (`GameModelsManager.GetGameModel` scans from the end). Siege: `GetConstructionProgressPerHour` via the model in force (Cheats' postfix neutral at its settings), storm rolls ~3.08/h by the camp leader's AI, `StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical` formula in `Forecast.Siege`; hunger wounds 10% of the garrison a day, never surrender. Battle: storm 1 round/h, others 2; strikes by `GetSimulationTicksForBattleRound` (never call it between rounds: the pool is empty then); TAOM's combat and power models. `Dashboard etacheck` checks the marching rule against real arrivals (Oct 16: 2.5% median error on marches of 12 h+, against 8.8% for distance/speed).
- **Moves in a battle, arrivals, raids (0.7.5, docs/TOOLS.md "What the first evening on 0.7.4 taught"):** a `SetMove*` given to a party in a `MapEvent` is taken but the party moves only once the battle ends (tools test `in_battle`). A lord's `MoveTargetPoint` (Tx, Ty) is often a waypoint short of the holding it heads for (a DefendSettlement lord walked straight to a point 8.6 ahead, 96.5 by road from the castle; a released lord to a point 7 ahead of a 488-long march): time an arrival along the way only when it ends within 5 of the holding or its gate. A raid with no defenders loots the village when `SettlementHitPoints` reaches 0, taking (sqrt(raiders) + 5) / 900 an hour (`RaidEventComponent.Update`, `DefaultRaidModel.CalculateHitDamage`; TAOM's `TaomRaidModel` adds feats and career passives). Battle simulation (`MapEvent.SimulateBattleRound`, `DefaultCombatSimulationModel`): a round every 60 minutes in a storm, 30 otherwise; each strike's damage (0.5 to 1) x 40 x (power ratio)^0.7 x advantage x morale factor knocks the troop down when a random number below its MaxHitPoints (100 for troops) is under it. The Hollin Gate storm ran at about 40% of the rules' expected defender blows and 150% of the attacker blows (power 1.57 against 1.93 by the model), so battles under way are run ahead at measured rates.
- **A holding's gate is not its centre (0.12.1, docs/TOOLS.md "Arriving at a castle"):** `Settlement.GatePosition` lies a median 2.9 from a castle's `Position` and 3.3 from a town's (up to 7.1; villages 0). A lord sent close to a holding stands by its gate, so every "has he arrived" test measures to the nearer of the two (`World.Near`); measured from the centre, three sieges stood 6.2 to 7.8 off for up to 15 days in run 20261025-1524-C2.
- **The one door to an AI storm (0.7.4, docs/TOOLS.md "Siege control"):** `MobilePartyAi.GetBesiegeBehavior` turns the camp leader to `AssaultSettlement` only when `BesiegerCamp.IsReadyToBesiege` holds: `IsPreparationComplete` and then the private `StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical()` (not inlined; the defenders inside not shy of a fight, militia and garrison, halved with the player inside, against every camp party's `CalculateCurrentStrength`, then the ram/tower terms). So a prefix on it decides every AI storm and is never asked before the preparations are done; the player's own storm menu does not go through it. Storming needs no engine built: the player may storm the moment the preparations are done.
- **After a battle, and the quick reaction (read in 1.4.8 for 0.19, docs/TOOLS.md "Harass, reserve and the battle facts"):** `MapEvent.FinalizeEvent` raises `CampaignEvents.MapEventEnded` with `RetreatingSide` still set, so the side that retreated can be read there (`BattleEnds`). `DisorganizedStateCampaignBehavior.OnMapEventEnd` slows every party of a battle with losses (`MobileParty.SetDisorganized`, 6 hours by `DefaultPartyImpairmentModel`, -0.4 in the speed sum by `DefaultPartySpeedCalculatingModel`) except the side that retreated; leaving an army and a sally do it too. `MobileParty.IsDisorganized` and `DisorganizedUntilTime` are plain public reads. The quick reaction is `DefaultMobilePartyAIModel.GetBestInitiativeBehavior` with `CalculateInitiativeScoresForEnemy` (TAOM registers no `MobilePartyAIModel`): search 9 around the decider, friends of each side within 6 of the target (in full within 3, the window narrowing as the decider closes in), the target's friends at 0.9, allies of other kingdoms for neither; `MobilePartyAi.GetBehaviors` acts on a score above 1. A lord's `Aggressiveness` is 0.9 + 0.1 Valor - 0.05 Mercy (`LordPartyComponent`). A party joining a siege camp while its leader is in a map event (a storm, a relief's attack) is put on that event's side (`EncounterManager.StartSettlementEncounter`).

## A besieged place's food, and the lords' (1.4.8; read Nov 13 for 0.19.5)

- `Town.DailyTick` (internal, once a day per castle or town, called from `Campaign.DailyTickSettlement` on the periodic settlement ticker, spread over the day): `FoodStocks += FoodChange`; below 0 it is set to 0 and the place's party gets `RemainingFoodPercentage = -100`; above `FoodStocksUpperLimit()` it is cut to it.
- `DefaultSettlementFoodModel.CalculateTownFoodStocksChange`: income is the lands (15 a town, 10 a castle) and each normal village ((hearth level + 1) x 6) plus buildings, none of it under siege; consumption is prosperity/40 plus every member of the garrison party/20, with perks and buildings; food sold to the town's market that day (`Town.SoldItems`, category `BonusToFoodStores`) is added to its store.
- `Settlement.IsStarving` is `Town.FoodStocks <= 0`. A starving place's garrison loses a tenth of its regulars a day (`DefaultPartyHealingModel.GetDailyHealingForRegulars`, via `SettlementHelper.IsGarrisonStarving`); a starving party outside a place loses a quarter of its regulars a day, and morale (`FoodConsumptionBehavior`).
- A party's food: one food item is one food (100 in `RemainingFoodPercentage`); each day it eats its `FoodChange` (`FoodConsumptionBehavior.PartyConsumeFood`), at random among its food items, then slaughters livestock. In an army, a starving party takes food from the army party whose food lasts longest (`TotalFood / |FoodChange|`), the cheapest item first, and the giver gains a little influence. Lords inside a place never eat its store.
- 0.19.5 (`SiegeFood`): a prefix on `Town.DailyTick` makes a besieged place take its shortfall from its lords' food by that same sharing rule, once its day would leave the store below one.

## A realm that loses its last holding, and its clans (1.4.8; read Nov 24 for 0.24.0)

- `FactionDiscontinuationCampaignBehavior`: when a settlement changes owner and the old owner's realm (never the player's) has no settlements left, the game ends that realm (`DiscontinueKingdom`): each clan's battles and sieges are finalized, each clan leaves for no realm (`ChangeKingdomAction.ApplyByLeaveByKingdomDestruction`, so `OnClanChangedKingdomEvent` fires with no new realm), then `DestroyKingdomAction.Apply`. The pack can veto it through the `CanKingdomBeDiscontinued` event.
- `DestroyKingdomAction` on its own destroys every clan still in the realm (`DestroyClanAction`), which is why the discontinuation empties the realm first.
- A clan in no realm with no settlement (not rebels, bandits or minor factions) is noted with a date 28 days ahead (`_independentClans`) and destroyed on its first daily tick past it, unless it joins a realm or gains a settlement first.
- Such a clan's daily look (`DiplomaticBartersBehavior.DailyTickClan`): about one day in five it picks a realm at random (a realm of its own culture weighs ten times as much as any other) and joins if the barter values it above 0, never the player's realm, one at war with it, or one it is at constant war with.

## The campaign map's clock and pace (1.4.8; read Nov 17 for 0.20.3)

- `Campaign.RealTick(realDt)` calls `TickMapTime(realDt)` every map frame: at play the campaign's step is `0.25 * realDt`, at fast-forward that times `SpeedUpMultiplier` (4, set back to 4 at each load; the cheat `campaign.set_campaign_speed_multiplier` changes it), nothing while stopped or while the main party waits in a stoppable mode. The step is kept as `_dt` (`Campaign.CampaignDt`), which every party's thinking and march, the sieges and the campaign events tick by, and the clock moves `4320 * step` seconds: 1,080 campaign seconds a real second, 18 in-game hours a real minute at play, 72 at fast-forward.
- So scaling the real seconds `TickMapTime` is handed slows the clock and every march together, while the map's visuals keep their own real time (`CampaignEntityComponent.OnTick` gets the untouched `realDt`). `TickMapTime` is a private method of about 140 bytes of IL, above the 100 the JIT inlines, so a prefix on it holds (module 0.20.3, `MapSpeedPatch`, settings.txt `map_speed`; the hooks' status line gives its IL size).
- What the game offers itself: only fast-forward's multiplier. Nothing slows play.

## Siege engines, walls and the storm (1.4.8; read Nov 16 for 0.20.0)

Read in the decompiled 1.4.8 source (`C:\BannerlordArchive\refsrc\TW148`) and checked against hourly copies of the Nov 16 session. None of the active mods changes these rules: TAOM's siege model only adds a trebuchet to the player's own defender list, and TFAOMFixes' fire-catapult patch fails to attach every session ("You can only patch implemented methods").

- **A besieger must stay set to besiege (Nov 24, for 0.24.2).** Every siege tick on the map (`SiegeEvent.Tick`, whenever neither the camp leader nor the place is in a battle) runs `BesiegerCamp.CheckBesiegerPartiesAndMakeThemLeave`: any besieger that is not the player's, not attached to an army, and whose `DefaultBehavior` is not besiege, escort or assault is taken out of the camp (`BesiegerCamp = null`, which sets it to hold). When the camp's last party goes, the siege ends (`FinalizeSiegeEvent`) with its engines and preparation. A new siege starts from nothing. Our carried-on orders set each lord to hold at a load, and the first siege tick then broke every siege of ours (the trace: `SetMoveModeHold` <- `RemoveSiegePartyInternal` <- `set_BesiegerCamp` <- `CheckBesiegerPartiesAndMakeThemLeave` <- `SiegeEvent.Tick`). Never give a besieging lord another move, even for a moment, unless he should leave.
- **Engines** (`Native\ModuleData\siegeengines.xml`): trebuchet 1,360 damage, 3,200 hit points, hit chance 0.58, a shot every 144 minutes; onager 640, 1,500, 0.62, 103 min; fire onager 768, otherwise as the onager; ballista 160, 500, 0.52, 72 min (never aims at walls); the defenders' catapult 440, 1,200, 0.28, 103 min; fire catapult 528. Damage is the same against walls and engines; a besieger shooting at engines loses 0.05 of hit chance a wall level (`DefaultSiegeEventModel`). Ram and tower 5,000 hit points, never shot at in the campaign. Building speed a side: the square root of its healthy men over the engine's man-days times 24, with perks and the defenders' buildings.
- **Spots**: the besiegers have 4 for throwing engines and 3 for rams and towers, the defenders 4 (`SiegeEnginesContainer`). The container takes any engine of its kind in any spot, but the map draws a camp's ram only in the first of the three and its towers only in the other two (`SettlementVisual.AddSiegeIconComponents`: the ram's spot indexes the scene's ram markers, one, and a tower's its spot less the ram markers): a ram in another spot throws IndexOutOfRangeException in the map's drawing (Nov 16, under 0.20.0). The game's own strategies list the ram first, so it always lands in the first spot.
- **One engine at a time**: preparations first; then only the first engine in a spot that is unfinished and not being redeployed gets the side's progress (`SiegeEvent.ConstructionTick`); the game's own strategy starts nothing while one is half built. An engine moved to reserve gets redeployment 0 and needs 2 hours (0.5 an hour) back in a spot; a half-built one moved there and brought back is never finished (redeploying stops building, and only built engines redeploy), and the side builds nothing more: a trap the drill never springs and clears if it finds it.
- **Bombardment** (`SiegeEvent.BombardTick`): every working (built, not redeploying) throwing engine in a spot fires once each reload. A besieger picks the defenders' engine nearest its spot (priority 7 less the spots between them plus 3) and the standing wall stretch nearest it (priority 5 less the distance plus 2, the stretches at 0.5 and 2.5), and between them at random weighted by those priorities (`BesiegerCamp.GetAttackTarget`); a defender shoots only the besiegers' nearest working throwing engine (`Settlement.GetAttackTarget`). Engines in reserve or half built are never aimed at. A destroyed engine leaves its spot free.
- **Walls**: two stretches in every castle and town, 30,000 / 50,000 / 67,000 hit points together at wall level 1 / 2 / 3, never mended during a siege (`DefaultSettlementGarrisonModel.GetMaximumDailyRepairAmount`).
- **The game's storm decisions**: the strategy is drawn at random at the siege's start (`SetDefaultTactics`); each list (`DefaultSiegeStrategyActionModel`) is a ram, a tower or two and four throwing engines, each rolled out alone as it is finished. The storm test (`BesiegerCamp.StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical`, asked by the camp leader's AI about three times an hour once preparations are done) needs the besiegers over the defenders times the walls' worth to a power 0.8, which falls 0.02 an hour after the 4th day; it then scales by engines built (none with fewer than two) and falls with a half-built one. On Nov 16 Gondor's camp at Yânu and Zarkan's at Zamarzîr stormed with the walls whole (a ram, a tower and one trebuchet or onager), and the storms were still being fought three days later; nothing builds or shoots while a storm is fought (`SiegeEvent.Tick` skips its body while the camp leader or the place is in a battle).
- **The storm the game fights out** (`DefaultCombatSimulationModel`): the walls' worth is `(1 + walls) / (1 + engines)`, walls `4 + level - 1`, a quarter of that only once both stretches are fully down (a stretch partly broken counts for nothing); engines: 0.25 for a ram or tower, 0.16 a ram (0.24 improved), 0.16 / 0.24 for one / two towers, 0.08 each plain throwing engine, 0.12 each fire one, counting only finished engines, in reserve too; the defenders' engines never count. While more than 30 defenders hold a castle (100 a town), they strike `round(0.5 + max(1 + n^0.3 * worth * 0.7, (n+1)/(m+1))) * 2` times an hour, the besiegers `round(1.5 + n^0.3) * 2`: the walls set the besiegers' losses, the defenders' number the storm's length. At Torech Emel (420 defenders, every engine built) that is 30 strikes an hour with the walls up and 14 with both stretches down.
- **A storm the player fights in person** (`CampaignMission.OpenSiegeMissionWithDeployment`): only built, working engines in spots go into the battle (`GetPreparedAndActiveSiegeEngines`), a stretch at 0 is a breach men walk through, and every ram and tower is broken after it. A thrown stone's hit damages everyone within 1.2 m of it, within 2.8 m and half as hard again for a big burst (`Mission.MissileAreaDamageCallback`, the burst's size chosen inside the engine).
- **The player's siege screen** builds, moves to reserve and scraps by the same container calls, for a side he leads (the game gives it the strategy Custom, which holds).

## Memory on the campaign map (1.4.8; read Nov 21 for 0.22.1)

- The game lets go of the engine's resources no longer in use (`Utilities.ClearOldResourcesAndObjects`) and of its unused portrait and banner pictures (`ThumbnailCacheManager.Current.ClearUnusedCache`, in TaleWorlds.MountAndBlade.View.dll of the Native module) only in `GameStateScreenManager.OnPushState`/`OnPopState`, for a game state that is not a menu: a battle or scene, the inventory, party, clan or banner screen. `MissionState` also calls the first when a mission ends. Nothing calls either on the campaign map.
- So the game's reserved (private, committed) memory climbs for as long as one stays on the map. Nov 20 (TAOM's `[MemSample]`/`[MemStation]` lines, every 30 s and at each screen): 14 to 24 GB in an hour of a test run, about 0.3 GB a real minute with the clock running, while the working set stayed at 7 to 9 GB and the managed heap (`heapMB`) under 1.3 GB. Leaving the banner screen took it from 20.7 to 15.7 GB at once; leaving a battle, 21.7 to 13.8.
- The game's ordinary full collections (gen 2, about one a minute on the map) free none of it: across 30 sessions of health.csv, a map minute with one grows about as much as one without. The big drops all came with a burst of 6 to 8 collections in one minute, at a screen change.
- Most of the gap between reserved memory and working set is probably the graphics card's (Windows charges a process's video memory to its commit); module 0.22.1's health.csv logs the process's graphics memory (`gpu_dedicated_mb`, `gpu_shared_mb`) to tell.
- Our module creates no engine objects, pictures or models (its only visuals are the lieutenant's window). Since 0.22.1 `MemoryTidy` makes the game's two calls on the map once the reserved memory has grown `memory_tidy_mb` since its low point (1,500 while the clock stands, `memory_tidy_running_mb` 3,000 while it runs), at most every 3 minutes, and logs what each freed (`memory/tidy`).
- **What the memory is (Nov 23, read live at 21-23 GB, about 1.5 h after a load and an hour after the storm of Caras Laerolin; scratchpad `memscan` and `HeapLists --types/--roots/--statics/--scenes/--distinct`, all read-only):**
  - **About 6.5 GB sits in ~330 blocks of about 32 MB, 1% of it in RAM.** They are committed but never written to, plain read-write and not on 4 MB boundaries, beside write-combined graphics upload blocks. Their commit rises and falls with the process's graphics memory (correlation 0.83 over 100 minutes; a tidy that took 1.4 GB off the card took 1.5 GB out of these blocks). So it is very likely the graphics driver's system-memory backing for video memory: every GB of textures and models the engine keeps costs a GB of the laptop's commit limit. The Nov 20 crash came at that limit (66.4 of 67 GB, a failed render texture).
  - **The engine's own allocator is mimalloc**, built into TaleWorlds.Native.dll. It is version 1.6 or 1.7 (options `page_reset`, `reset_decommits`, `segment_cache`, `eager_commit_delay`, `reserve_os_memory`; read from the environment as `MIMALLOC_<OPTION>`). Its 4 MB segments, on 4 MB boundaries, held 6.7 GB, 55% in RAM. In that version, freed memory on Windows is reset (`MEM_RESET`) unless `reset_decommits` is set, so it may keep its commit. Untested. EOSSDK carries its own copy.
  - **The last battle stays alive on the map** until the next one: the `Mission` (ended, its scene field cleared), 5,571 `Agent`s, formations, teams and the siege handlers. Holders found:
    - TFAOMFixes `Trims._puCands` (the battle and 3 soldiers) and `Trims._aim` (a dictionary keyed by siege weapon and soldier, 1,867 soldiers);
    - DismembermentPlus `DismembermentPlusMissionLogic._dismemberment` (2 soldiers);
    - the game's own `ScriptComponentBehavior._prefabScriptComponents` and `DotNetObject.DotnetObjectReferences`: 16 `LadderQueueManager`s whose `_userAgents` still list soldiers. The engine still refers to them, so the ladders' scene objects live on.

    Any one of them keeps the whole battle. "Not tied to the card" memory (private minus graphics) went from 7.2 to 12 GB during that battle and stayed.
  - **The map's tracks and the player's siege circles make a new `Decal` wrapper every frame:** `MapTracksVisualManager` and `SettlementVisualManager.TickSiegeMachineCircles` call `GetComponentAtIndex(0, Decal)`. Seen: 854,178 wrappers on 602 decals. They are finalizable garbage (finalizer and GC churn, about 25 MB), not a leak of engine objects.
  - **Even paused in a menu, memory climbed about 2 GB every 3 minutes,** in the ~32 MB blocks, and each tidy took it back. What makes those graphics resources while paused is not yet known.
  - Our `MapExport._scene` keeps the `MapScene` in a static field: harmless within a campaign, but it would hold an old map after loading another save. Clear it when the campaign ends.

## The campaign map's way-finder (TaleWorlds.Native.dll 1.4.8; read Nov 18 to 20 for 0.21.0 and 0.21.1)

The full reading, with addresses, is in docs/PLAN-ways.md ("Phase 2 notes"); the copy is `src/Common/NavCopy.cs`.

- A party's way (`MobileParty.ComputePath` → `GetPathBetweenAIFaces` with region switch costs) is engine binding 941. With radius 0.3 it runs: a straight line first unless the start face is flagged 8 or special; else A* over the walking mesh's faces (first reach final, crossing points on doorways narrowed at closed corners by min(0.49 or 0.99 x the edge, 0.3), step = distance + both faces' extra cost x 0.5 x 2 + region switch cost).
- Then the way is drawn: a funnel through the corridor's doorways, narrowed by min(0.4 x the edge, 0.3) at closed corners; then every bend the straight line from the bend before it reaches is dropped, the line walked face by face through any open faces, not only the corridor; then a point at each land and sea change. Only the bends are handed back, the end last.
- So the game's way can leave the searched corridor, and bends less than a funnel through it. A line may not cross a face with extra cost or a costly region switch.
- Corners count as closed by their flag 0x80 (the mesh's edge) or the bit the engine gave the party's set of closed ground when it was first used (bit 1 on this map). Each face's edges are listed counter-clockwise; an edge between two faces is kind 2.

## Storms joined from outside, and lords without a party (1.4.8; read Nov 28 for 0.25.7)

- **A relief that joins a storm** (`MapEvent.AddInvolvedParty`): a party not in the settlement joining the defenders of a siege assault turns it into `SiegeOutside`; the settlement's own party keeps leading that side, so it fights with no leader's edge and none of the lords' own captain's bonus, and the walls count for nobody. A party that attacks the besiegers before any storm (`StartBattleAction.Apply`: the defender has a `BesiegedSettlement`) starts a `SiegeOutside` it leads itself. Run 20261127-1453-C2, day 160: Goksun's storm of Shúrdmúl began at day 160.4; the agent's own strike order sent 17 lords at his camp at 160.5; they reached the walls about 160.75 and joined the storm, the side was led by the castle (its blows measured at 118% of the rules, Rhun's at 150%), and all 17 were taken. Since module 0.25.7 a lord whose order would ride into a storm under way holds (`Runner.StormJoinHeld`), except with relieve.
- **The garrison beside a relief** (`SallyOutsCampaignBehavior.CheckSallyOut`, every 4 hours and at each battle's start): it sallies when its strength plus the settlement's own realm's parties within the joining radius (any not in a settlement, aggressiveness above 0) exceed 1.5 times the enemies there while the besiegers fight a battle outside the walls (2 times otherwise), strengths by `GetCustomStrength(PlainBattle)`; it joins the side attacking the besiegers, and the battle becomes a `SallyOut`.
- **Lords without a party** (`HeroSpawnCampaignBehavior`): a freed lord (fugitive or released) is moved once a day with a 3 in 10 chance, an active one whenever another of his realm's holdings scores 10 or more, to the best-scoring holding (`GetMoveScoreForHero`: a town +100 over a castle, his clan's own town +500, +100 for each clan-mate waiting in a town, three times for where he already is; none under siege or of a realm at war); never out of a besieged place (`CanHeroMoveToAnotherSettlement`). A clan raises a party for one only while its score passes 100 (`CalculateScoreToCreateParty`: fiefs x 100 less parties x 100, a hundredth of its gold, 200 with no party), so a realm that loses holdings keeps its freed lords waiting longer. When the holding falls they are taken. In run 20261127-1453-C2 all 39 of the kingdom's lords taken in its 14 lost holdings were such lords, several two or three times (Luda at Dale, Mount Gundabad and Mount Gram); left to the game (Nov 28 decision): no change.
