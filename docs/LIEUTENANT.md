# The lieutenant (since 0.10)

An agent that serves the player's own realm, through the game's chat box on the campaign map. It is the human-in-the-loop arm of the project: every proposal, and whether the player agreed, changed it or turned it down, is logged.

**What I want from it.**
- Oct 22: an agent that acts as the player's lieutenant. The player types orders into that chat to their lieutenant, which then orders lords to do things on the map. It would propose sending x, x, x lords to a place, with a combined force of x men, to defend it. I would verify and approve the order. I could also ask it what lords are doing across the realm, any threats that were spotted on fronts I'm far away from, and also what lords I gave orders to are currently doing.
- Oct 23: everything stays verbal in the chatbox. My approval should also be verbal. Its answers are short and concise whenever possible, and popups pause the game until the popup is closed; it may command every lord of the realm, including my own clan's parties; it stays in the chat window for now, and the companion chat will be a quality of life upgrade for later.

## Using it (since 0.10.1: its own window)

Oct 23, after the first try in the chat log: a button should work as a shortcut (say Enter) to go into the lieutenant pop-up automatically and chat with it through that pop-up rather than through the game's own log. This way it's easier to approve, disapprove, or comment on its proposals as well (we could have buttons for each).

- On the campaign map, press **Enter** to open the lieutenant's window (since 0.10.2; **T** opens the game's own chat box, as in the game; Oct 23: Enter should open the lieutenant window, T should open the chat window). The game stands still while it is open (`lieutenant_pause` in the bridge's settings); the clock runs again as it ran when it closes.
- The window shows the conversation (everything since the game was loaded, up to the last 200 lines, newest at the bottom), the line to type in (up to 400 letters; **Enter** sends), and **Close** (or **Esc**).
- What is typed and not sent stays when the window is closed and is back in the line when it opens again, until it is sent (since 0.13.19; Nov 1: sometimes I look at the map to name settlements as I write my prompts, which means I have to close the menu while writing them; `LieutenantWindow._draft`, this game session only).
- **Scrolling** (since 0.10.3; Oct 23: I should be able to scroll up and down to see the full answers of my lieutenant and the chat's history): the mouse wheel over the conversation, or its bar on the right, scrolls back through it, as in the game's chat log. It opens at the newest line and follows each new one, unless you have scrolled back to read, when it stays where you are (your own words always bring it to the end). A proposal's list scrolls the same way when it is long, and what you approve stays in the conversation ("You approved:" and the orders).
- Ask anything: what lords are doing, what the realm can see on a far front, what your ordered lords are up to, who could reach a place first, how a battle would go. The answer comes in 5 to 40 seconds, and shows at once even with the game paused.
- Ask for something to be done ("hold Goblin Town against the orcs with dwarves"): when you tell it to act ("give the orders now"), it gives them; when you ask for a plan, it proposes who goes, with how many men and when they get there, and the proposal shows in the window. **You answer it in words** (since 0.13.8; Oct 29: I don't want to have to click "approve" or "decline" to approve the lieutenant's orders: I should only have to approve them in the chat, since many times I approve some but have comments on others): yes to all of it, to some ("send Dáin and Nori, but not Thorin"), or yes to some with changes to others; it gives what you agreed to and proposes the rest again, changed, in the same answer. Nothing it thought of itself moves until you agree. The Approve and Decline buttons are gone.
- Orders are given on the game's next tick, as every agent's are, so they start once the window is closed.
- A longer answer (when you ask for one) follows its words in the window.
- When the window is closed, its answers and what becomes of its orders show in the game's message log too, and wait in the window.
- **Your own army** (since 0.13.9; Oct 29: the lieutenant should be able to form an army for me, under my name, so for example if I ask it to get every lord stationed at Imnagath to join my army, it should be able to form an army for me and automatically have them join it): the order `my_army` with the lords to call (and, if you name one, where it gathers). - **A fresh report, even straight after loading** (since 0.13.9): the lieutenant's report is written every in-game hour, and the game loads paused, so words said before the clock first ran used to reach it with the report the session before had written (Oct 29: it still could not see Stein's army's lords, from the report written before the fix and the load). Now, until the first hour, each of your messages copies the realm as it stands and writes the report first.
- **Lords in armies** (since 0.13.8, Oct 29: the lieutenant could not see lords in armies): the realm's report names every lord marching in each of the realm's armies, and yours, under its leader. An order of the lieutenant's to such a lord takes him out of the army first (once the order has passed its checks). Breaking up an army hands its leader and every lord in it back to the game's own AI, and the receipt names them, so the lieutenant gives each an order of his own in the same answer; otherwise they are no longer under its orders (Stein and Górli, Oct 29).
- **The conversation is the save's** (since 0.11.1; Oct 23: the chat I have with my lieutenant should be persistent within a save, even if each prompt is technically a different session. ## How it works

- **The folder** (outside the repository, made by `scripts\setup-agent.ps1 -Workspace <folder> -Kind lieutenant -Mode live`): `agent.txt` with `kind = lieutenant` and `realm = player` (the player's realm at each load), its instructions `warroom-system.md` (from `agent/lieutenant-system.template.md`), its war room's settings `warroom.txt` (effort medium, no council; since Oct 29 no thinking cap and no time limit on the lieutenant's thinking, after a 64-order proposal was stopped at 120 s; the game is paused while its window is open), its channel `warroom\`, and its memory `memory\<campaign>\chat.jsonl` (the conversation). It is listed in the bridge's `agents`, after the realms' agents, and `scripts\agents.ps1 start` runs its war room with the others.
- **The window** (`LieutenantWindow.cs`, `module\GUI\Prefabs\LieutenantWindow.xml`): a screen layer of the game's own UI on the map screen, in the look of the game's text pop-up (its canvas, title, divider, buttons and typing area; its `TextQueryParentWidget` focuses the typing line when it opens), with `LieutenantVM` as its data. Opened by one Harmony patch (`ChatHook`, since 0.10.2) on the game's chat box's look at its keys each frame (`GauntletChatLogView.HandleInput`, in Native's GauntletUI, found by name): on the campaign map with a lieutenant and the chat box shut, Enter there (read from the map's own keys, as the chat box reads it) opens the window instead; T, and Enter with the chat box open, stay the chat box's; while the window is open the chat box does not look at its keys. (0.10.1 hooked the chat box's start of typing, `MPChatVM.StartTyping`: two lines, which the game folds into its callers, so the hook attached but was never reached, and Enter and T opened the chat box. I saw it on Oct 23.) Each frame (`SubModule.OnApplicationTick`) it opens on request, closes when the map is gone, sends on Enter and closes on Esc; it locks the clock while open. Answers come in on the frame's queue (`Background.ToUi`), so they show with the game paused; orders go to the game's tick (`Background.ToMain`). The patch attaches only when a lieutenant is in the agents' settings. (0.10.0's chat box: the game's single-player chat log has its typing line taken out, though its `TextInputWidget` still names it; that version put it back with a prefab of the same name and five patches on `MPChatVM`.)
- **Approve and Decline** (`Lieutenant.Approve`, `Decline`): the button carries out the proposal waiting as it stands (or drops it), and the next message tells the war room what the ruler did ("notes" in `chat.json`, WHAT YOUR RULER DID SINCE YOUR LAST ANSWER in its message).
- **The bridge's side**: the player's realm is a seat like an agent's realm (its sight, its report every hour with a **YOUR RULER** line saying where the player's party is, its look-ups every hour, its view file for the war room's questions), but it is **never poked**. What the player sends goes into `chat.json` (the words, any words since the last answer, and the proposal waiting, if any) and a line in `chats.log`.
- **The war room** (`LieutenantRoom.cs`): for each new `chat.json`, one fresh call: its instructions and the order library as the system prompt; as the message the conversation so far (the last 14 exchanges), what became of its orders lately (`receipts.log`), the waiting proposal, the realm's report, and the player's words. It may read its look-ups and ask the same questions as the realms' agents (`ask_the_way`, `battle_odds`, `can_catch`, `roads_in`). The answer goes to `orders\chat-N.json`: `say`, `propose`, `carry_out` with `orders`, `drop`, `detail`; the war room adds `pending_chat`, the waiting proposal's number from `chat.json`.
- **Never unasked** (`LieutenantRules.Gate`): the bridge carries out orders only when the answer's `pending_chat` is the proposal still waiting, i.e. the player's words were the answer to it. Orders without that become the proposal and wait for a yes; a new proposal replaces the waiting one; `drop` forgets it.
- **Its orders** go through `Orders.FromAgent`, like every agent's, in its mode (live) and for every lord of the realm (`lords = all`), never the player's own party. Since 0.11.1 a lord holding a Party AI Controls order takes the lieutenant's instead (0.10 refused him). A refusal is said in the window at once; the order's call-backs and its end come into the window from the realm's hour (`Lieutenant.Heard`).
- **The conversation of a save** (0.11.1, `Lieutenant.Keep`, `Restore`): every line (the player's words, the answers and longer texts, Approve and Decline, the proposals, orders given on the player's word, refusals, what became of the orders) goes into `data\<campaign>\lieutenant\chat.jsonl`, labelled with the session, the in-game hour and the wall clock. At a load the lines of the save's history are kept (`History.HoldsLine`: the chronicle's rule, plus the save's wall clock, since most of this conversation happens with the clock stopped, so a line said after a save in the same paused hour is not that save's; saves' notes carry the wall clock since 0.11.1, older notes go by the hour). They go back into the window, and the last proposal not answered before the save waits again. `chat.json` carries the latest 60 (`history`, `history_total`), `conversation.txt` in the channel the whole conversation; the war room uses them instead of its own memory (`memory\<campaign>\chat.jsonl`, still written as a log, which mixed saves: the first two chats of Oct 23, from a later date, came into TEST START's). Nothing of it is in the save.
- **Party AI Controls** (0.11.1, `PacLink.cs`, docs below in "Party AI Controls"): every order of the lieutenant's shows in Party AI Controls' panel as that lord's order; `party_ai` sets Party AI Controls itself.

## Party AI Controls (since 0.11.1)

Party AI Controls is the player's own command panel for his clan's parties and (with TFAOMFixes' "I have orders for you") his vassals. Its members are internal, so `PacLink` reaches them by name (checked against the installed 1.4.5 DLL, Oct 23: `PartySettingsManager`, `Settings(Hero)`, `IsHeroManageable`, `_partySettings`, `_partyTemplates`, the settings' `Order` with its private setter, `OrderQueue`, `FallbackOrder`, `PartyTemplate`, `SetPartyTemplate`, `Composition`, the options, `PAICustomOrder(IMapPoint, OrderType)`, `PAICustomTemplate(string, TroopRoster)`, `PartyCompositionObect(float x4)`; and TFAOMFixes' public `VassalOrders.Commanded`).

- **The lieutenant's orders, shown.** Our order layer carries them out, as every agent's (so its orders and the agents' are carried out the same way, for the comparison runs). When one starts for a lord of the ruler's realm (`Orders.StartTool` → `PacLink.OnOrderStarted`), the lord's Party AI Controls order is replaced by the nearest of its kinds (`PacRules.MirrorOf`: besiege → Besiege; defend, relieve → Defend; garrison, rest → Stay; patrol, screen, a watch at a holding → Patrol around; goto, sell → Visit; recruit → Recruit; engage, hunt → Attack; follow → Follow), or by none (raid, shadow, a watch post on open ground, hold, leave, gather, join: Party AI Controls has no such order). His queue and backup order stay. The receipt says which order it replaced.
- **No tug of war.** While it runs, Party AI Controls passes him by: its hourly look (`KeepHeldPatch`, a prefix since 0.11.1) and its check at a holding's gate, which ends visit and recruit orders there (`PacEntryPatch` on `PartyAIThinker.OnSettlementEntered`), both skip him (`PacLink.Skip`). So it neither carries out nor ends the order it shows, nor starts his backup order, a recruiting trip by itself, or a dismissal meanwhile.
- **When it ends** (`Orders.End` → `PacLink.OnOrderEnded`): the mirror comes off, and the next order of his queue starts there (Party AI Controls carries it out), or none, and then his backup order once he is free and out of an army (Party AI Controls' own rule).
- **The ruler wins.** If the lord is given another order in Party AI Controls while the lieutenant's runs (the panel, or the "I have orders for you" talk), the lieutenant's order ends at once ("Party AI Controls now holds another order for him"), on Party AI Controls' next look or our next hour. The gap of 0.11.0 (two owners pulling each hour) is closed.
- **party_ai** (the lieutenant's only, in its library's own section; `PacLink.Apply`, checked whole before anything is set): `order` (a Party AI Controls order carried out by Party AI Controls itself, replacing whatever he does, the lieutenant's own order of ours included; "none"), `queue` (a list; [] empties it), `fallback` (his backup order; "none"), `template` (by name; "none") or `new_template` {name, troops} (made from top-tier troop ids, as Party AI Controls' own "create template" makes one, and given to him), `composition` (troop mix in %), `options` (every per-lord option of the panel: `join_armies`, `donate_troops`, `take_troops_from_holdings`, `besiege`, `raid_villages`, `take_lord_prisoners`, `recruit`, `recruit_from_enemy_holdings`, `buy_horses`, `horse_budget`, `auto_recruit`, `auto_recruit_below`, `dismiss_unwanted`, `dismiss_above`, `max_troop_tier`, `patrol_radius`), `managed: false` (a vassal handed back to the game's AI, out of Party AI Controls). Its orders: defend, patrol, patrol_lands, stay, besiege, follow, attack, recruit, visit. It goes in a proposal like any order and waits for the ruler's yes.
- **Who it manages.** Party AI Controls manages the ruler's clan's lords, and vassals only while they are on TFAOMFixes' list of commanded vassals (or all vassals, if its kingdom parties switch is on); its panel lists only those. A vassal the lieutenant commands is put on that list, as the ruler's own talk puts him, and a new entry gets auto-recruit and troop dismissal off, as that talk's do. While managed, Party AI Controls' options and troop mix apply to him (its recruiting filter among them), as for the vassals the ruler commands himself. When the lieutenant's order ends and nothing of Party AI Controls' is set for him, he comes off the list and his new entry goes, so he is as he was. Anything set with `party_ai` stays, saves included: that is the ruler's and the lieutenant's on purpose, and Party AI Controls keeps it in the save, as it keeps the ruler's.
- **Saves.** The mirrors, and the entries and list places made only for them, are taken out just before every save and put back after (with the orders' own hand-back), so a save never holds them: nothing of ours is in a save, and after a load the lieutenant's orders are gone like every agent's. The lieutenant's own `party_ai` orders are recorded in `data\<campaign>\lieutenant\party_ai.jsonl` (labelled like the conversation), so the runs and the recorder still know them for the lieutenant's after a load.
- **The record.** The recorder no longer takes the lieutenant's Party AI Controls orders for the player's own (`PacLink.IsOurs`, `WasOurs`); a test run's battles say "lieutenant (Party AI Controls)" for a lord on one of its `party_ai` orders.
- **What the lieutenant sees.** Each message carries PARTY AI CONTROLS NOW (`PacLink.Overview`, read on the game's thread when the player sends words, not every hour): every managed lord's order (and whose), queue, backup order, template, troop mix and the options unlike the defaults, who is not managed, the templates with their troops, and the top-tier troops of the realm's peoples a new template can aim at.
- **Logs**: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\BannerlordAgents\logs\warroom-lieutenant` (`calls.jsonl` with kind `chat`, each call's prompt, stream and answer), the channel's `chats.log` and `receipts.log`, and the session's trace (`lieutenant` events: said, answer file, answered with its gate).

## Tools, diplomacy and letters (since 0.13)

Oct 28: the lieutenant should be able to write tools, based on my permission, and the player should be able to communicate with their allies (who are agents) through their lieutenant.
- **Its proposals** carry, beside its orders, tools it would write (`tools`), diplomatic acts (`diplomacy`) and letters to your allies (`letters`), all under its yes rule: carried out on your words answering the proposal, or your Approve; dropped on Decline. The proposal's box shows each tool with what it does, its settings and whether the game's checker passes it, each act, and each letter's first lines. A load puts a waiting proposal back whole.
- **Tools** go into your realm's library, the one your kingdom's own war council keeps too ([TOOLS.md](TOOLS.md#the-agents-own-tools-since-013)); it has `check_tool` beside its other questions. Since 0.13.22 (Nov 1) its instructions carry the tool language itself (The tool language: the form, the moves, the names, the functions and the limits, as the realm agents have them, with a tool's call-backs told in your window); until then it was told only to copy the library's tools, so it knew nothing of words such as `food_days_at`. A smoke test checks that both instructions list every move, name and function the language has.
- **Diplomacy** for your realm: a proposal is added to your kingdom's votes and waits for your decision in the kingdom screen; the game's own windows bring you the offers made to your realm ([DIPLOMACY.md](DIPLOMACY.md)).
- **Letters**: to an ally whose ruler is an agent; your allies' letters come into its window (green) and into its next message (LETTERS FROM YOUR RULER'S ALLIES), so it tells you what each says and asks.
