# Tools: what the agent's lords can do between its answers

Since version 0.4 (Oct 8), every order the agent gives is a **move** or a **tool**:
- a move is a single step, such as go to a holding or attack a lord;
- a tool is a small set of rules that a lord follows on its own between the agent's answers. Defend, for example, patrols around a holding, shelters from armies too strong for the lord, strikes weaker ones and breaks weak sieges.

A tool calls the agent back, with a poke, only when its plan may need to change. This is the plan's behavior language (RQ1, RQ2): our tools come first, and the agent's own tools come in week 4.

## How an order travels

1. **The agent answers a poke** with one JSON object (the war room writes it into `orders\poke-N.json`; [WARROOM.md](WARROOM.md)):
   ```json
   {"poke": 12, "orders": [
     {"lord": "lord_MM2_3", "do": "defend", "at": "town_MM2", "radius": 25, "why": "Aragorn marches on Western Moria"},
     {"lord": "lord_MM10_5", "do": "gather", "at": "castle_MM5", "lords": ["lord_MM8_1", "lord_MM14_3"],
      "then": {"do": "relieve", "at": "town_MM2"}}]}
   ```
   - `at` is a holding and `target` a lord, both as ids or names. `lords` is a list.
   - Settings are numbers, given at the top level or in `"settings"`.
   - `then` is the next order, started when this one finishes.
2. **The game checks each order** against the game as it stands (`Orders.Issue`, on the game's thread):
   - the lord leads a party, and is not marching in someone else's army;
   - the place or target is the kind the tool takes (an enemy castle, one of your holdings, ...);
   - the settings exist and are kept to their ranges;
   - for an army, the game's own rules and influence price (below).

   In shadow mode it stops here and receipts "checked".
3. **The lord is held:** the game's AI stops deciding for that lord. The tool goes to the **runner** on the helper thread (`Tools.Runner`).
4. **Every in-game hour, right after the hourly copy,** the runner checks each tool's rules against the realm's own view (`Tools.World`): what the realm sees, and where it last saw an enemy. It never looks at the game itself, so a tool knows exactly what the agent could know.
   - When a rule fires, the tool changes state and the lord gets a new move, made on the game's thread (`Orders.ToolMove`).
   - A rule may call the agent back. That becomes a notice, so it goes out with the same hour's poke.
5. **On the game's thread** the move is re-issued whenever the game turns the lord away. The hold ends when the lord dies, is captured, loses the party or leaves the realm, and the agent hears why.
   - **Party AI Controls** (since 0.6.3): each hour it looks at every lord party (`PartyAIThinker.OnHourlyTickParty`), and for a lord it does not manage that stands still (`Hold`) with the game's AI switched off, it switches the AI back on: it takes a held lord for a stuck one. The game's own hourly decision then changed the lord's plan within the hour, and the order was re-issued (Oct 16: 40 times in two and a half game days, mostly army leaders waiting for their lords). A hook right after that look (`GameHooks`, `KeepHeldPatch`) holds such a lord again when one of our orders holds it.

6. **Across a save and a load of it** (since 0.13.5, `CarryOn`): the orders live in memory only and every load clears them, so at every save each tool run is written down beside the save's history note (`data\<campaign>\orders\<save>.json`: the very definition it runs, its order, settings, state and the hour it entered it, remembered values, the move in force, the safety and food rules' places, its record so far), with the agents' wage caps. A load of that save at that hour holds each lord again under the same tool run's id once he passes the checks of step 2 (alive, free, leading his party in the realm, obeying its agent, which must be the same folder and live), and the runner takes the tool up where it was and gives its move again; the agent's first look says how many carried on and which could not, and why. An order given after the save is not in it, as nothing else of those hours is.

## Three rules every order carries

Relief (since 0.13.15) is checked first each hour, then safety, then food.

- **Relief.** A held lord rides with all haste to relieve a lord of his realm or an ally's in a fight (`Runner.Relief`, `ReliefPlans`, `CanRide`). Mert, Oct 30: "lords under any order and not in an army should always ride with all haste to relieve lords in a fight if their combined strength beats the enemies' in the area's combined strength for the expected duration of the fight ... Armies should still try to relieve lords if they are close enough to reach them before the fight is done and combined strength is stronger than enemies' in the area's combined strength, but should never break up because of this". Before it, no order did: a guard struck enemies near its own holding only, and every other order left a lord in a fight alone.
  - **The fights:** a battle in the field, at a siege camp (`siege_outside`) or over a raid, with a lord of the realm (or an ally's, in view) on one side and an enemy realm's on the other. Not a storm or a sally behind walls.
  - **How long it lasts:** the battle clock (`Forecast.Measured` once it has run two rounds, else `Forecast.Battle` at middling morale), at most `Library.MaxReliefHours` (24). A fight the clock does not see ending within five days counts as 24 hours. Since 0.13.16 (`Runner.EndsIn`) the clock runs twice: as it stands, and then with the enemies who could join it within those hours coming in as each would arrive (the joiners below); the shorter is used.
  - **The riders:** the held lords of the realm who can get there before it ends with time to spare (since 0.13.17 not all of them: "Who is sent" below), at his own pace as the crow flies (`World.HoursTo`). An army's leader rides with his whole army (its strength, its pace); the army is never broken up.
    - **Time to spare** (since 0.13.16, `Library.ReliefSpare`): a fifth of the fight's hours left, at least an hour. The clock is a forecast.
    - **Who is sent** (since 0.13.17; Mert, Nov 1: "make sure the logic assigns enough lords to tip the balance for relief, and that lords that have a defend/patrol/recruit order (so lords that are basically not doing anything important) still go to relieve another lord regardless of how the battle is going, since a crushing victory reduces losses compared to a close victory"):
      - Nobody, while our side with every lord who can get there would not outweigh the enemy.
      - Else every lord **free to ride** (`Runner.FreeToRide`: defend, patrol, hold, recruit, rest, and a realm's own recruiting tools, any with "recruit" in its name), however the fight goes.
      - And lords on **any other order** only while the lords in the fight would not win alone, under `Library.ReliefWinBar` (1.5) times the enemy with its joiners. They go nearest first (one already riding to it first), until our side reaches 1.5 times the enemy; the rest keep to their orders.
      - Why: on Winter 4-5 (Nov 1) Dol Guldur's Dramthak, about 7,000 strong and gathering for Sirannon, rode 17 hours to relieve Vugra (2,731) against Kisha (about 450).
      - The agent's call-back says why each lord goes: to win bigger (free, the fight won alone), or to tip the balance (another order).
    - **Who does not ride:** a lord marching in another's army (he goes where his army goes); one in a fight already; one camped in a siege (leaving would lift his part of it); one inside a besieged castle or town (he cannot leave); one on his way to buy food with under two days of it.
  - **The weighing:** our side is each party in the fight on our side (each by itself: an army's lords fight party by party) and every rider. Theirs is each party in the fight on theirs, and every enemy lord and army in view who could join it within its hours (`World.Joiners`); when theirs is a bandit kind, also every band of that kind in view who could join (since 0.13.16, `World.BandsOf`: bandits join no lord's fight, but they do join one their own kind is in). Our side must be the stronger.
  - **Why the two 0.13.16 changes** (run 20261029-1903-A, Winter 2, Nov 1): Osven (139 men) was beating a band of Mirkwood Stalkers (118) near Düglar-mauz, three hours in, when the rule sent 16 lords 16 to 24 hours away on a clock of 24 hours or more (our side 3,844 against 976). Five more bands came in, 577 men by noon; the clock shortened hour by hour, all 16 turned back within three hours, as the rule says, and Osven was taken 13 hours after they set out. The bands that sank him were never counted, nor run into the clock.
  - **The ride:**
    - Each rider rides to the nearest such fight he can get to, striking the enemy side's leader (else its strongest party).
    - He does so whether he was sheltering or not; the safety rule does not run while he rides.
    - The agent is told once a fight (who, where, when he gets there, how long the fight should last, both sides' strengths, how many lords ride and how many enemies could join). Since 0.13.16 the place reads "fighting Mirkwood Stalkers 2 north-west of Düglar-mauz" (`Runner.FightPlace`), not "... the spot 2 north-west of ...".
    - In the fight, the order waits.
    - Once the fight is over, no longer outweighed, or out of his reach, the order resumes.
  - **Every realm:** the same for every realm's held lords, rival agents' included. The library tells the agents so (`Library.Text`).
  - **Not covered:** lords on the game's own AI (the game decides for them), and lords holding a Party AI Controls order.

- **Safety.** The game switches off a held lord's quick reactions, so that lord does not flee by itself. (One reaction survives, the game's own exception: a held lord may still attack, or pull back from, an enemy fighting at or besieging the very holding it was sent to; `MobilePartyAi.GetBehaviors`.)
  - When the enemy lords, armies and bands of bandits (since 0.6.1) within `danger_radius` (default 8) of the lord, counted together (since 0.12.2) with every enemy lord and army in view that could reach him within the order's `join_hours` (since 0.12.3, default 8; below), are `caution` times stronger (default 1.5), the lord shelters, or runs (since 0.6.2, Mert: shelter where the defence would hold, else run):
    - **Where it may go:** the castles and towns of the realm and its allies not under siege that it reaches before the enemy (its hours there at its own speed, no more than the enemy's at the enemy's), nearest first; the order's own holding first when it has a `stay_inside` setting.
    - **Would it hold?** For each, the game's own test for whether besiegers should assault (`BesiegerCamp.StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical`): they assault only when stronger than the defenders times the settlement's advantage to the power 0.8. Taken with a ram and two towers built, the walls are worth 2.4 times the defenders for level-1 walls, 2.8 for level 2, 3.2 for level 3. The defenders are the garrison, the militia (militia hold the walls, though they never sally), the realm's lords inside, the realm's lords holding a defend or garrison order for it within 20, and this lord. Against them: the danger, and every force threatening that place (the threat near it, below). An ally's garrison counts only for a place in sight.
    - **Shelter:** inside the first that would hold, even the castle the enemy is marching on.
    - **Run:** if none would, toward the nearest it reaches first that takes it farther from the enemy, never the place the enemy besieges or marches on, stopping outside; with none, straight away from the enemy, twice the danger radius. It runs again from where it is each hour, and takes a refuge that would hold as soon as one is in reach.
    - **Already inside** a castle or town that would not hold (and not besieged, so it can still leave): it leaves and does the same. Inside a village, which gives no protection, it is as if outside.
    - `stay_inside` 1 (garrison's default) keeps the lord in its own holding whatever comes.
  - While an order strikes a siege camp (relieve, defend's relief), the other lords camped in that siege are part of the fight it chose and do not count (0.6.1).
  - The order resumes once the danger has passed (at least two hours, and nothing that strong within 1.5 times the radius).
  - The agent is told, and `caution` 0 switches this off. Since 0.6.1 defend carries it too, beside its own sheltering.
- **Food.** With under two days of food, the lord goes to the nearest friendly town or village to buy more, then resumes.

## The library

The agent reads it in every call as `tools.txt` (written from the same code; `agents.tools` prints it in the game).

| Group | Orders |
|---|---|
| Moves | goto, patrol, engage, follow, leave, recruit, supply (0.13.19), hold, free |
| Tools | defend, garrison, besiege (its relief alarm counts lords too since 0.7.2), raid, hunt, screen, rest, watch, shadow (0.7.1) |
| Armies | gather, relieve, join, add, release, disband |
| Changing orders | a new order replaces the old one; adjust (settings only); no orders at all is a valid answer; then |

What each one does, takes, sets and calls back on is in `tools.txt`. The definitions are the JSON files in `src/BannerlordAgents/Tools/Library`, built into the DLL.

### Food for a holding, and men from the clan's garrisons (since 0.13.19)

Mert, Nov 1:
- "I'm seeing a lot of castles at my fronts with zero food due to raids on their villages, and I think agents and the liutenant should be able to order lords to donate extra food to settlements to make sure they can outlast sieges." Then "Lets do that": the lord buys first, and every realm's agents get the order.
- "lets also allow orders to get lords to go to their clan's settlements and take troops from those garrisons as they recruit up until the strength of the garrison falls to a floor set by the agent."

**The game's food rules** (1.4.8, `DefaultSettlementFoodModel`):
- A castle or town's daily change is its lands (+15 a town, +10 a castle), each unraided bound village ((hearth level + 1) x 6) and its buildings, less its people (prosperity / 40) and its garrison (men / 20).
- Food items sold to its market that day add one each (the `BonusToFoodStores` categories). Castles have no market.
- Under siege nothing comes in. The stores hold at most 300 (a castle 450, more with a granary: `Town.FoodStocksUpperLimit`).
- No game action hands food to a holding. Lords buy food for themselves in towns and unraided villages (`PartiesBuyFoodCampaignBehavior`: `PartyFoodBuyingModel.FindItemToBuy`, `SellItemsAction`).

**supply** (a move, `Supply.cs`, verb `supply`):
- **What the place needs:** enough to last `days` (default 10) of siege at what it eats then (people and garrison), at most its stores' limit, less what it has.
- **What he can spare:** what he carries beyond `keep_days` (default 3) of his own men's eating (`MobileParty.FoodChange`).
- **Buying first:** with too little to spare (once an order), he rides to the market costing the least detour on the way, and buys there as the game's lords buy food. That is a town not besieged and not an enemy's, or an unraided village not an enemy's, with food for sale, within four town gaps. He keeps half his gold and stays within `InventoryCapacity`.
- **Handing over:** inside the holding, one food item goes into its stores for one day's portion (the market's rate), up to its limit.
- **Ends:** handed over, the place needing nothing, nothing to bring, or the place besieged or lost before he gets in. A besieged place is not supplied: the food would have to pass the besiegers.
- The safety rule and the relief rule ride along as with every order. Supply is not free to ride, so he relieves a fight only to tip the balance.
- The report shows each own castle's and town's food ("food 64 of 450, about 2 days under siege (it eats 32 a day)") and its garrison's strength, never an enemy's (`SettlementRow.Food/FoodCap/FoodPerDay`, `ViewHolding`).

**garrison_floor** (on every move and tool, like caution; `Library.GarrisonFloor`, default 0 = takes none; `RecruitTour.GarrisonGives`, `FromGarrison`):
- **Where:** at a castle or town of his own clan, not besieged.
- **Who it applies to:** a recruiting lord, meaning the recruit order's tour or a recruit stop (`recruit at`, which defend's and patrol's refills use).
- **What he takes:** healthy men from its garrison, the highest tier first, one at a time. They follow the recruit order's rules: any kind under 80% of his ranks, then his clan's own troop lines, within his wage cap, never past his ranks' limit.
- **How far:** as long as the garrison's strength (the game's estimate, worked out again after each man) stays at or above the floor.
- **The tour:** it counts such places as stops even with no volunteers there.
- The settings go to the game's thread with the order (`HandOrder.Settings`) and across a reload (the carry-on file's `settings`).

**Since 0.13.20** (Mert, Nov 1: "I also like all the optional extras you proposed"):
- **The hunger warning** (`Brain.LowFood`, a poke of kind `low_food`, in each realm's hourly look). It fires for an own castle or town that is not besieged, has food for under `LowFoodDays` (3) days of siege, and has an enemy lord or army in view within `LowFoodNear` (40). It names the nearest enemy and the strength near. It is told again after a day, or sooner once its food has fallen by a day more (`Memory.HungerTold`).
- **The order language** reads an own castle's or town's food: `food_at(h)` and `food_days_at(h)`, the days of siege it lasts. Both are none for a village or another realm's holding (its food is not known).
- **Doctrine principle 19,** "Victual your castles before the siege" (docs/DOCTRINE.md).
- **The realm agents' instructions now name these:** the shared settings line names `garrison_floor`, the moves list has `supply at X`, the functions list has `food_at` and `food_days_at`, and Recruiting describes the garrison floor.

### The recruit order tours (since 0.9.2)
Mert, Oct 22: a lord with a recruit order from an agent "also follows the logic as my own parties". The same rules as his own parties' recruit orders and the computer's lords (both in TFAOMFixes); the agents' copy is `RecruitTour.cs`, on the game's thread:
- **Where:** the order's place (a town, castle or village of the realm or an ally; castles give recruits in this pack), then the nearest town, castle or village of the realm, an ally or a realm at peace with it, within reach (four times the usual gap between neighbouring towns), that the order has not swept in the last day; one after another. The move is `tour`, and the stop it is bound for is the order's place.
- **What he takes:** every volunteer his standing with the notable reaches and he can pay for, by the game's own gold and wage rules, with no dice, until his ranks are at 80%. Above that, only men of his clan's troop lines: the troops of the clan's party recipe, what grows into them and what they grow into. Once he is full, a man from outside them goes for each one taken (lowest tier, wounded first). Purchases go through the game's own (`GetRecruitVolunteerFromIndividual`), so with TFAOMFixes loaded he gets his own people's troops (its `Offers`, asked by name what that will be).
- **The end:** his ranks full and all of his clan's lines, or nothing left within reach to recruit. It ends on the game's side, and the agent hears why with the next poke (`order_ended`). It no longer stops after three days at one place, and a stop that falls or empties is simply passed over.
- **Why he could not buy (since 0.9.3):** when volunteers were within reach but none could be paid for, the end names the commonest bar instead of "nothing left": his wages at their cap (`wage cap`: wages a day of the cap, which the game sets each day from the gold of his clan's leader: under 30,000, 200 to 600 a party; 30,000 to 90,000, 600 to 1,200; above, none; half again for the leader's own party), his gold below what the game keeps a lord from recruiting under, or his clan's purse (`no gold`). On Oct 22, 13 of Dol Guldur's 14 tours ended in their first hour at 3 to 43% saying "nothing left", with 1,500 volunteers waiting: 21 of its 47 lords were at their wage cap, and the agent sent the same lords again.

### Money (since 0.9.4)
Mert, Oct 23: "The caps should be the agent's call. It should be able to estimate the value of loot and prisoner ransom money from a battle and decide accordingly. An understanding of economics should be given to it now", and "make sure that an order to sell actually sells loot and just loot, instead of important food and other things".
- **The game's rules** (1.4.8, read in its code; told to the agent in its instructions' "Money" section): each lord pays his men every day from his own purse; his clan's leader tops him back up to 5,000 while the leader has 4,000 or more; a lord above 10,000 hands a tenth of the excess to his clan daily (`DefaultClanFinanceModel`); unpaid wages cost morale and men desert. A party at its wage cap cannot recruit; the game sets every computer war party's cap each day from its clan leader's gold (`MakeClanFinancialEvaluation`). Computer lords sell everything but food and good riding horses on entering a friendly town (pack animals too) and hand prisoners over on entering a friendly castle or town; a soldier fetches a quarter of his recruit price, a lord much more (`DefaultRansomValueCalculationModel`).
- **YOUR PURSE** in the report (and in full in the council's briefing): each clan's gold and the game's reckoning of its change a day; the lords at their wage cap; the caps the agent set; loot and prisoners carried (worth and ransom); the realm's battle takings. Each lord's look-up adds his gold, wages and cap, loot, prisoners and last sale.
- **wages** (a plain order like adjust): `cap` a number of denars a day, or target `none` (no cap) or `game` (the game's own again); with `lords`, the same for those lords. The cap is put back right after the game's daily setting and checked hourly (`Wages`), handed back to the game around every save (a party's cap is saved with the game) and cleared at every load.
- **sell** (tool, at a friendly castle or town): rides in, and the sale is made on entering; it calls back with what it got (`sale_text`). A lord already inside sells at once. **Loot only** (`Purse.IsLoot`): weapons, armour, shields, ammunition, harness and plunder (goods that are not food); never food, horses (riding, war or pack), herds, books or banners. For every lord holding one of our orders, the game's own sale on entering a town is replaced by this one (Harmony prefix on `PartiesSellLootCampaignBehavior.OnSettlementEntered`), so recruit tours and every other order sell loot only too. Prisoners are handed over by the game's own sale, measured around it.
- **A battle's worth**: around the game's handing out of a battle's results (`MapEvent.CalculateAndCommitMapEventResults`), each winning lord of a watched realm's loot and prisoners are counted before and after, and the enemy's fallen shared out among the winners by men (`TakingsPatch`), into the campaign's `takings.jsonl`, read back at each load (`Takings`). With 3 won battles on record, `battle_odds` adds to a win what it would bring in: fallen x loot per fallen man, prisoners per fallen man, ransom per prisoner, all from the realm's own battles.

### The chase question, before a hunt (since 0.9.3)
Mert, Oct 23: hunting an enemy faster than the hunter is "completely unnecessary" when there is other work; it is worth it only with nothing else to do, or to drive the enemy away from something, and the agent should be able to ask an enemy's speed before a hunt order.
- **`can_catch`** (war room, beside `ask_the_way`, `battle_odds` and `roads_in`; `ChaseTool.cs`): one of the realm's lords or armies (`hunter`) against one enemy its report shows (`quarry`). Each side's pace by the game's rules (the enemy's as the map shows it, or as when last seen, or its trail's), the way between them and the hours to reach where it is, then: faster, by how much, reached in so many hours if it holds still and run down in about `hours x hunter / (hunter - quarry)` if it flees over the same ground; about as fast or slower, it is reached only when it stops or turns toward the hunter, and how much it gains an hour. When it is marching somewhere, who gets there first. From the realm's own view file only.
- **hunt's text** in the library now says to weigh the two paces first, and when a faster target's hunt is worth setting (nothing better to do, or keeping it from a place or lord it threatens). The instructions' "Asking the way" section names the question, so it is there only in calls that have it.
- **The refill stop** that defend and patrol make (the single-stop `recruit` move) stays one stop, but buys the same way there.
- A hand order (`agents.vassal ... recruit <place>`) tours the same way.

### Armies, by the game's own rules
- **gather** forms the army at once with the game's own call (`Kingdom.CreateArmy`), at the meeting place the agent names. The called lords march there and join as they arrive. The tool judges the army from the next hour on, since the hour's map copy was taken before the army existed.
  - **Lords too far away to be called yet** (since 0.6) are not left out: each gets a **join** order and marches toward the army until the game would let it be called (below). The army then forms with whoever is in range, even the leader alone, and gather waits for the marching lords too (`army_joining`) before it calls back that the army has formed.
  - **Lords who cannot come at all** (in a battle, inside a besieged holding, besieging, already in an army) are left out and named in the receipt; the order is refused only when no lord can come (since 0.5.1).
  - The receipt says who comes now, who marches first (with men, and the influence they would cost if called now; less as they come nearer, since the game prices distance), and who cannot come.
- **join** (since 0.6, `target`: the army's leader, or a lord in that army): the lord follows the army's leader and joins the army as soon as the game's calling rules allow (`CannotCall`, the same as gather's), paying the leader's clan's influence then, like add. Checked on the game's thread when the move is made and every in-game hour (`Orders.TryJoin`).
  - It keeps marching while the lord is only too far, or busy in a battle.
  - It fails, sets the lord free and calls the agent back when the army has broken up, its leader is gone, the lord cannot be called for another reason, or the leader's clan cannot pay. An army missing in the first 3 hours is waited for, since it may be forming in the same answer.
  - A lord who joins goes into the chronicle without a poke (as does a held lord called into an army); gather's own call-back tells the agent when the army is complete.
- **add** sends lords too far away to be called the same way: a join order each.
- **Influence:** the leader's clan pays the game's influence price for each lord (`CalculatePartyInfluenceCost`). The order is refused if the clan cannot afford the lords called at once; the receipt warns when it may not afford the marching ones too.
- **Who cannot be called** (the same as the game's AI):
  - a lord already in an army, in a battle, besieging, or inside a besieged holding;
  - the realm's ruler (except as the leader);
  - a lord farther away than the game allows (`MaximumDistanceToCallToArmy`: eight times the average distance between neighbouring towns, along the way): these march to join instead.
- **Who cannot lead:** a mercenary clan's lord.
- **Called lords we were holding** are let go, so the game can march them to the army.
- **An army's lords by name** (since 0.13.8; Mert, Oct 29: "my liutenant is not able to see folks in armies"): the report lists, under each of the realm's armies (and under the ruler's own, in the lieutenant's), every lord marching in it with his id, men and strength (`ViewParty.Members`, `Views.ArmyMembers`); before, only the army's size, so no lord in it could be named in an order. `disband`'s receipt names the leader and every lord it hands back to the game's own AI. The lieutenant's order to a lord in an army takes him out of it first, once the order passed its checks (`Orders.LeaveArmy`, as `release` does); a realm agent still orders the leader (`release`, `disband`). The game's own AI never calls a lord we hold into its armies (`DefaultArmyManagementCalculationModel`, 1.4.8: `DoNotMakeNewDecisions` parties are skipped), so a held lord is not taken from an order that way; one held by nothing (on Party AI Controls with joining armies allowed, or on the game's AI) can be.

### Patrol and defend (since 0.6.1)
- **The game's own patrol** (`SetMovePatrolAroundSettlement`, read in 1.4.8): the lord walks to random points in a ring between half and all of a patrol radius from the holding's gate, and the radius is half a day's march at the lord's own speed for a castle or town, a quarter of a day for a village (`DefaultMobilePartyAIModel.GetPatrolRadius`). At the orcs' median speed, 3.4 map units an hour, that is 20 to 41 units out from a castle, 10 to 20 from a village.
- **A patrol kept within a radius:** a move made by a tool with a `radius` setting (defend, screen), or the plain patrol given one, is our own patrol instead: from point to point, each a reachable spot between a quarter and three quarters of the radius from the gate (`Orders.NextPatrolPoint`), the next one picked the hour the lord reaches the last. The plain patrol's radius is 0 by default, which leaves it to the game.
- **defend**, step by step:
  - **patrolling** within its radius; each hour, the first that holds of: the holding lost (it stops); ranks below `floor` while it is not needed there (it goes to refill; below); a siege it can break (it strikes the besiegers, with the defenders inside when they would sally out); any other siege (it shelters); with `stay_inside` 1 at a castle or town (since 0.11.6), enemy lords and armies due at the holding, by the game's marching rules, within the hours it would take to ride back from the edge of its patrol plus six (`relief_within(at, radius / max(pace, 0.3) + 6, radius)`), at least `shelter_at` times its strength (it goes inside ahead of them, caution or no caution); the threat near the holding at least `shelter_at` times its strength (it shelters); a weak enemy lord near the holding (it strikes the weakest). The same early rule breaks off a strike. Why (run 20261024-2110-C2, Bagbûrz, Winter 17): Gárjan's army of 12,300 was told to hold the castle from inside (radius 8, `shelter_at` 1, `stay_inside` 1, caution 0), patrolled outside its walls, went in only when Lorgath's 16,700 came within 8 at 08:00, and was caught 5 from the gate at 12:00; caution 0 had switched the wider danger rule off, as it should;
  - **sheltering** (0.6.2): inside the holding when its garrison, militia and the realm's lords there, with this lord, would hold it against the threat near it (the game's assault test above), even under siege, to reinforce its walls; otherwise in the nearest castle or town of the realm or an ally that would hold against what threatens it, or else the nearest other one. `stay_inside` 1 shelters inside the holding whatever the odds. It strikes a siege from there when it becomes breakable, and patrols again after at least 3 hours once there is no siege and the threat is below 80% of the sheltering line (with `stay_inside` 1, the force due before it could ride back too);
  - **refilling** (since 0.6.3, Mert: lords weak alone may be needed, and massed they are strong; recruit when not needed urgently): `ranks` is the lord's own party against its size limit, and the orcs' lords stood at a quarter of it on average (Oct 16), so the old rule sent nearly every defender, armies too, off to recruit the hour it got the order. Now it goes only when it is not needed where it is:
    - not in an army (an army's leader never leaves to refill by itself: the agent massed it for a reason);
    - the holding not besieged, and no enemy lord or army within twice the radius of it (`enemy_threat_near`: bandits do not count);
    - not within two days of leaving a place that gave few recruits.

    **Since 0.13.4** (Mert, Oct 29: "Sure, lets do that", to refilling at the castle it guards now that castles recruit in this pack), a guard of a castle or town first takes the holding's own volunteers (state `restock`, the move `recruit at`, no call-back): back to its patrol once its ranks are over the floor (after at least 3 hours); still short 2 hours after arriving, it rides on to the nearest town or village (`nearest_supply()`, with a call-back), or, when that is the holding itself or none, stays there under the refill rules below; it breaks off when an enemy lord or army comes near or the holding is besieged, and gives up after a day without reaching it (back to its patrol, trying again in two days). A guard of a village goes straight to the refill below.

    It refills in the nearest town or village of the realm or an ally that can give recruits, and is back on the first of: its ranks over the floor (after at least 3 hours); an enemy lord or army near the holding, or a siege of it (it breaks off, and the patrol's rules decide what next); 12 hours at the place without 5 points of ranks gained (it leaves, and waits two days before trying again); two days in all. `floor` 0 never leaves to refill;
  - **striking a weak lord**: given up when the target is gone or out of sight, too far from the holding, too strong, when another siege starts, or when the threat near the holding reaches the sheltering line;
  - **joining the ruler's own attack** (since 0.13.11; Mert, Oct 29, chasing Mauhur, 686 men, with 3,865: "allied lords around me are still not engaging him and are instead running from him. They should be engaging him normally since they know I am around"): the 13 Lasgalor guards had struck Mauhur the hour he did, and broke off together three hours later when Mauhur ran more than their radius (20) from the holding, each guard's leash; none fled. Now the realm's ruler's own party attacking an enemy in person (`ruler_target()`, `World.RulerTarget`: the ruling clan's leader's party, set on EngageParty, at an enemy) changes three things: a guard within its radius of that enemy joins the attack when the enemy's company is within its side's bar; the guard's leash grows to three times its radius from the holding while the ruler keeps it up; and that enemy with its company (`World.RulerHunts`, when the ruler's force is the stronger) counts neither as a threat near the holding nor as a danger to flee. A lord attacking the same target now counts on our side (`World.Friends`), so the ruler's army weighs in the strike's bar. The same for every realm: a rival agent's guards join their own ruler's attacks.
  - **breaking a siege**: until it is broken, the besiegers grow too strong, or other enemy forces come near beside them.
- **The threat near a place** (`threat_near`, since 0.6.1): the strength of the hostile lords, armies and bandits in view within the radius, and of the hostile lords and armies seen there in the last 6 hours, at the place they were last seen, unless known gone. Defend and raid weigh it when they decide to shelter or pull back; striking still needs an enemy in view. `enemy_threat_near` (0.6.3) is the same without bandits: the forces that make a lord needed where it is.

### Watch and shadow (since 0.7.1)

Mert, Oct 16: the agent screened round Western Moria to "find Aragorn's army after Hollin Gate". Sending a weaker lord to keep tabs on the road from Hollin Gate would have been smarter, but the agent had no order for it: every order that places a lord was tied to a holding. The game's own way there runs about 260 map units north round the mountains, though the two are 53 apart as the crow flies, and the screen never left the town's surroundings.
- **Spots.** A spot is a point on the map that is no holding, named by where it is: `p673_994`, map units east and north. The "ask the way" answers name spots: each crossing on the way, the narrowest place, and up to 6 points spread along it ([ROUTES.md](ROUTES.md)). A spot is geography, so naming one tells the agent nothing its realm could not know. The bridge checks it is ground a party can stand on (`NavigationHelper.IsPositionValidForNavigationType`), else takes the nearest walkable point within 4 (`GetAccessiblePointNearPosition`, with a note in the receipt), else refuses. Spots are exempt from the unknown-id alarm, and the orders in force name them ("the spot 4 west of Western Moria [p673_994]").
- **watch** (at: a spot or a holding): to the post (`post`: a point; at a holding, its gate, outside), then it stands there. It reports when it has taken up the watch, each enemy army within `watch_radius` (12) with its men, strength, distance from the post, what it is doing and where it is, and again when that army has gone out of sight. With `also_lords` 1 it reports enemy lords too. Its caution makes it fall back from anything too strong and return to its post once the danger has passed.
- **shadow** (target: an enemy lord or army): it keeps about `keep` (8) map units from its target, its point worked out again every hour: closing in when farther than keep + 2, stepping back to keep + 3 when nearer than keep - 2, standing in between. It reports at once, when the target heads for a new place (`toward_of`), and every `report_hours` (6). Out of sight, it goes to where the target was last seen (`toward`), and gives up after `lose_hours` (8). Its target is spared by the safety rule (it keeps its own distance) unless the target turns to chase it.
- **A trap found writing them:** a rule's `set` happens before its call-back is filled in, so a call-back naming a value the rule clears names the cleared value. watch keeps the departed army under its own name (`gone_army`) for that call-back.

### The game's clocks: marches, sieges and battles run ahead (since 0.7.3)
Mert, Oct 17: "We should be adjusting our prediction of the marching speed of a force based on the game's exact equations, so the ai has a good idea on where its own/enemies' armies will reach their waypoint. We should do the same about siege and battle timers so it can plan ahead." Three research passes read the 1.4.8 code, the decompiled TAOM 2.0.26 and the pack's other mods (CheatsForBannerlord, ImprovedGarrisons, TFAOMFixes); the rules below are the ones in force in Mert's game. Where a mod could change a number, the hourly copy reads it from the model in force instead of copying the rule.

**Which models are in force.** Several mods registering the same model: the last one registered wins (`GameModelsManager.GetGameModel` scans from the end; `CampaignGameStarter.AddModel` hands the previous one in as `BaseModel`). In Mert's load order: the speed model is TAOM's `TaomPartySpeedModel` (confirmed by the map export's `speed_model`); the siege event model TAOM's `TaomSiegeEventModel` (only its defender engines differ; building speed vanilla, with CheatsForBannerlord's postfix neutral at its settings); the combat simulation TAOM's (vanilla hits, then a cut only for TAOM refuges); military power TAOM's (tiers 1-6 vanilla, 7-10 fixed, mounted x1.2); ImprovedGarrisons' speed model is off in its configs; TFAOMFixes turns the player clan's own fiefs' defender engines into fire catapults.

**Marching** (`src/Common/GamePace.cs`, one file built into the module and the way-finder):
- The game's sum: base x (1 + every factor added into one sum), at least 1 (`ExplainedNumber`). The base is 4 x (200 / (200 + men))^0.4, every man of an army's parties pooled on its leader, wounded included, plus an overload penalty; its factors are horses, cargo, herds, wounded, prisoners, morale and perks. The hourly copy reads both from the game's own cache, `MobileParty._lastCalculatedBaseSpeedExplained` (`BaseNumber`, `SumOfFactors`): the very numbers the game moves the party with, so the pack's base rules are exact whatever they are.
- Then the ground and the hour, into the same sum: forest -0.3 (-0.15 with a Forest Kin scout and three quarters on foot); water, rivers, under bridges, bridges and fords -0.3; desert and dunes -0.1; night (22:00 to 02:00) -0.25; snowfall -0.1; the scout's Pathfinder, Desert Born, Night Runner, Day Traveler; and TAOM's peoples' bonuses for their ground (forest for Rivendell, Mirkwood, Lothlórien; snow for Erebor, Gundabad, the Goblins, the Misty Mountain Orcs; desert, plain, swamp; Mordor's night), read at load from the game's own feats (a feat whose id ends in `_forest_speed`, `_snow_speed`, ... with its `EffectBonus`).
- What the named rules leave unexplained in a party's speed now is measured hourly from its own speed (`_lastCalculatedSpeed`) and carried forward (`Pace.Other`). On Oct 16's frames the typical lord carried about +0.19 (a quarter below +0.155, a quarter above +0.26), constant for a party: most likely the scout's Uncanny Insight (+0.001 a point of Scouting above 200; TFAOMFixes raises lords' skills). The session summary samples it as `march:unexplained`.
- **A party covers its speed / 1.2 map units an in-game hour** (`Campaign.Tick`: 4,320 seconds of map time per unit of dt, the party moving speed x dt): 20 x speed a day. Measured on Oct 16's frames: 301 lone lords marching moved 0.81 of their speed in an hour (0.83 less corners). The old reckoning (distance over the speed number) was some 20% too quick; the old way-finder took the speed number as map units an hour and multiplied the ground in.
- **The arrival** (`Forecast.ArrivalOf`): along the way the game planned for the party (the hourly copy now keeps the whole `NavigationPath`, up to 128 corners, for every marching lord, not only with the dashboard on), the ground under each quarter unit from the walking map (`MapGround`, `ground.bin`), the night as it comes. The report gives it for each of the realm's lords marching to a holding or to a point of its order, and for each lord in view marching to a holding: "going to Western Moria, there in about 52 hours (Summer 12, 04:00)"; for one seen earlier, "if it kept on, there about ...". A party's march goes into the view file (`march`: base, factors, other, feats, perks, foot share), so the "ask the way" tool reckons the same way; an enemy out of sight with no march known goes at the pace its trail shows (`Pace.Flat`, all in).
- **Checked on the real hours** (`Dashboard etacheck`, Oct 16's load, 438 marches to a holding with their arrival seen, before 0.7.3's copy, so the base parts were worked out from the men): the game's rule was off by 4.2% at the median (+1.4% signed), against 9.0% (-4.9%) for the old sum and 6.8% (+3.0%) for the old way-finder; on the 145 marches of 12 hours or more, 2.5% (+0.5%) against 8.8% and 6.1%.
- The facts' and look-ups' travel hours, and the tools' `hours_to`, are now by road at the lord's pace over a whole day on open ground, night counted (`World.PerHour`, `GamePace.DayAverage`).

**Sieges** (`Capture.Sieges` hourly, `Forecast.Siege`):
- Copied live: the camp's preparations and each engine's progress, each engine type's building rate by the model in force (`GetConstructionProgressPerHour`: √(healthy besiegers) / (24 x man-day cost) x (1 + engineering)), the strategy, the walls' worth now (`GetSettlementAdvantage`), the engines counted as built and the most advanced unfinished one, the storm test's strengths (the defenders inside it counts, halved with the player inside; the besiegers; the garrison's part), the place's food and its change a day, the garrison's men and wounded, the besieging army's cohesion.
- Run ahead in steps of 0.1 hour for 10 days: nothing is built before the preparations (48 man-days); then the strategy's engines one at a time (prepare assault: ram, tower, tower, ballista, ballista, onager, onager; breach walls: ram, tower, 4 onagers; wear out: ram, tower, 4 trebuchets). The camp's leader rolls the storm test about 3.08 times an hour (`MobilePartyAi`, check interval 0.30 to 0.35 hours): ratio = besiegers / (defenders x walls' worth^e), e = (0.8 - 0.02 a hour past the 96th) x 1.25 without a ram x 1.25 without a tower; no storm at a ratio of 1 or less; the chance a roll = 0.1 x ratio x min(3, engines built) / 3 x (1 - 0.85 x (P + 0.25 x (5 - engines))^2), P the most advanced unfinished engine's progress, so the camp storms just after finishing an engine and hardly while one stands half built. Once the food is gone a tenth of the garrison's men lie wounded a day (militia do not eat; nobody surrenders), and the defence falls with them.
- Checked against the code's worked example (2,000 men, a level-1 castle, defenders 450, besiegers 2,600): preparations 25.8 hours, a ram at 32.2, towers at 40.8 and 49.4, a storm more likely than not by 49.8 hours (the research pass: about 50).
- The report's new **SIEGES AND BATTLES** section, with one line on how the game runs them, gives each siege of the realm's holdings, by its or an ally's lords, or in its sight: preparations, engines built and next, when a storm could first come, the chance it comes within one, two and three days, the hour by which it is more likely than not, the storm test's numbers, the food, and the besieging army's cohesion.

**Battles** (`Capture.Battles` hourly, `Forecast.Battle`):
- Copied live for every battle with a lord or a holding in it: its kind, place and start; each side's healthy men, wounded, losses so far, mean troop power by the model in force (`MilitaryPowerModel.GetTroopPower` with the side leader's modifier and the battle's context), battle advantage (`GetBattleAdvantage`), and morale weighted by men; the walls' worth.
- Run ahead round by round with the game's expected numbers: one round an hour in a storm, two in any other battle; strikes a round by `GetSimulationTicksForBattleRound` (a storm while the defenders are above 30 at a castle or 100 at a town: the attackers 2 x round(1.5 + D^0.3), depending on the defenders' number only, the defenders 2 x round(0.5 + max(1 + 0.7 x walls x D^0.3, (D+1)/(A+1))); else each side min(2 x the other's men, its own^0.6)); each strike knocks a man down with chance about 0.295 x (power ratio)^0.7 x advantage x the morale term; a side that loses a round (losses above 1.3 times the other's, the other's plus one, and 2% of its own men) loses 3 morale; a side led by a party routs at 0 (siege defenders, led by the holding, never do). Lords seen marching to the battle's holding join their side when they arrive.
- The realm's own side's morale is known; another's is not and is taken as middling (50).
- Checked: a storm of 960 against 4,500 strikes 28 times a round against 58 (the walls 3.18), and 2,050 attackers no more; run ahead, the attackers lose about 20 men an hour and break in about 14 hours. At Hollin Gate the attackers lost about 25 an hour.

### Siege control: the storm rule, the relief bars, and battles run ahead before they begin (since 0.7.4)
Mert, Oct 20, after the walk-through of Hollin Gate under 0.7.3, which left five gaps (no time for a storm or a field battle before it began; the agent sees only the relief its own lords see; the storm is the game's choice; an answer comes about 10 game hours after the alarm; the enemy's morale is guessed): "We should fix all gaps now. Gap 1 is the most important, and we should give time estimates both for siege battles and for field battles. Touching on 3: I wonder if there is any way we can give the AI a tool to dictate siege decisions, starting with the storm time. It can be gated on storm immediately, storm after x siege engines constructed etc. based on its understanding of the enemy garrison's strength and the imminence of relief." And for the fourth: "the agent should set the bar here - what relief force strength level forces a withdrawal, what relief force level forces an immediate storm? it should set the bar as it begins the siege, prematurely, and the logic should run as part of its siege tool."

**The storm rule (gap 3).** besiege has three new settings:
- `storm`: 0 the game's own commander decides (as before); 1 storm as soon as the preparations are done, `storm_engines` engines are built (0 to 7, default 1) and the besiegers are `storm_ratio` times the defenders (0: no bar), counted as the game's own storm test counts them; 2 do not storm (starve them out).
- While the order is in its siege stage, each hour the tools' worker hands the rule to `StormGate`, by the besieging lord's party. A Harmony prefix on the game's storm test (`BesiegerCamp.StartingAssaultOnBesiegedSettlementIsLogical`, private, too big to be inlined) answers from the rule for that camp; a camp with no rule, or rule 0, is left to the game's own test untouched. It is the second hook that changes what the game does (the first: `KeepHeldPatch`, which holds again a lord Party AI Controls wakes; CLAUDE.md). The self-check "storm rule" fails when it did not attach.
- **No storm before the preparations are done, whatever the rule:** the game asks the test only then (`IsReadyToBesiege`: `IsPreparationComplete` first), as it holds the player to it too. The player may storm with no engine built, so rule 1 with `storm_engines` 0 and a relief bar may too.
- The rule is cleared when the order leaves the siege stage, ends, is replaced, or the tools are reset (a new load). The log says each change of the rule and each time the hook's answer flips (`storm` events: rule, storm, hold, with why: "1 of 2 engines built", "besiegers 3,400 against defenders 900").

**The relief bars (gap 4).** Two more settings, set when the order is given and acted on the hour they are met, with no wait for an answer:
- `withdraw_on_relief`: once the relief due within `relief_hours` (24) reaches this many times the besieger's strength, it lifts the siege and marches into the nearest castle or town of the realm (`nearest_fort`); once inside, the order ends with the new ending `stop`: the lord is held where it is, and its next order does not start ("Its next order does not start; give it one."). With nowhere to go it holds where it is and says so. This bar is weighed first.
- `storm_on_relief`: once that relief reaches this many times its strength, it storms as soon as the camp may (the rule's `Now`: storm at the next check once the preparations are done, engines or not), and the call-back says when that is ("The camp's preparations are done in about 12 hours (...); it cannot storm before.").
- "Due within" is by the game's marching rules: each enemy lord or army of the relief (`relief_coming`, near the place or heading for it, in view or seen so in the last 6 hours) gets an arrival: along its planned way when it heads for the place (`Forecast.ArrivalOf`), else its distance at its pace over a day; one seen earlier is moved on by the hours since. Lords already inside the place are not counted (the storm test counts them as defenders). The relief alarm's call-back and the relief list now give each one's arrival: "Faramir, strength 900, 60.0 away (due in about 23 hours), defending Fornost".
- New functions: `relief_within(at, hours, radius)`, `prep_hours(at)` (hours until the camp's preparations are done; 0 done), `storm_text(at)`.

**Battles run ahead before they begin (gap 1).** The game's battle rules (0.7.3) are now one file built into the module and the war room (`src/Common/GameBattle.cs`), run for battles that have not begun:
- **A storm begun now**, in each siege line of SIEGES AND BATTLES: who would take or hold the place, in how long, with how many left, and the losses an hour, counting the lords seen marching to the place as they would arrive (on the defenders' or the besiegers' side). The besiegers' and defenders' men are copied with the storm test's strengths (`AttackerMen`, `DefenderMen`), and the besiegers' morale. Example (the smoke test): 2,086 besiegers against 194 defenders behind a level-1 castle's walls: about 60 hours (60 to 67 as the unseen morale is low or high), the besiegers taking it with about 1,734 left; with two lords of 2,000 men each seen marching in, due in 6 and 12 hours, the defenders hold.
- **The storm rule in the siege clock:** with a rule set, the clock runs it instead of the game's chance: rule 1 storms at the first step the engines and the bar are met (2,000 men at a level-1 castle, one engine: the ram, at 32.2 hours), rule 2 never; the line says so ("Your storm rule: do not storm; by it the camp storms not within 10 days").
- **A field battle** in the facts, under each enemy the poke is about: the realm's nearest lord alone, and its three nearest together, against it in the open: "If they fought in the field, by the game's battle rules (two rounds an hour; nobody leaves a battle once in it): Ugluk alone (500 men) would beat it, it retreating in about 13 hours (5 to 18 hours as their unseen morale is low or high), 351 of yours still standing and 45 of its 300 left (it gets away with its lords)". Since 0.11.6 every battle run ahead (these facts, the `battle_odds` question, the report's battles under way) also says what the beaten side has left of what it brought, and that a side which retreats gets away with its lords (nothing is captured in a retreat): in run 20261024-2110-C2 the agent called a forecast "narrow" that kept 79% of its men and left the enemy a third of his.
- **The `battle_odds` question** (war room, beside "ask the way"): `ours` (the realm's lords or armies), `theirs` (enemies its report shows), optional `at` (an enemy castle or town to storm, the camp's preparation time given first, a ram assumed built; or one of the realm's own, their lords storming it); lords seen marching to `at` join as they arrive. From the realm's view file only.
- **The field retreat:** the game lets the side losing a field battle retreat after 8 rounds at morale 20 or less with 4 rounds lost in a row; the run gives it 5 rounds more after that, then 4 of pursuit, and says "retreating".

**Morale (gap 5).** The realm's own lords' morale is copied (`PartyRow.Morale`, and its leader's Tactics); the other side's is not seen, so every battle run ahead is run at 30, 50 and 70 and given as a range, the middle one first.

**What the realm can know of a relief (gap 2).**
- **ENEMY REALMS, AS THEIR ENCYCLOPEDIA PAGES SHOW ANY RULER:** each realm at war with it: its lords (`Kingdom.AliveLords`) and the strength of all their parties together, as the kingdom's encyclopedia page shows anyone, beside the part of it in sight now. So the agent knows how much of the enemy it cannot see.
- **The `roads_in` question** (war room): the roads into a holding from the castles and towns of the realms at war with it (who holds which place is on the map for anyone), found as the game finds ways: where they come together, a spot on each `hours_out` (12) of a lone lord's march out from the walls (clustered within 10), the hours of warning a watch there gives a lone lord and an army of 2,000, and which places each road serves.
- The frontier's enemy castles now count only the lords inside that are in sight, beside the garrison and militia.

### What the first evening on 0.7.4 taught (since 0.7.5)
Oct 20, the save besieging Western Moria. Naglur's besiege order (storm 1 at twice the defenders, no engines; `withdraw_on_relief` 1.2) stormed Hollin Gate at 07:50 by its rule (3,457 against 312). At 13:47 its withdrawal bar was met, and the order announced "lifts the siege, marches into Cerin Amroth" and gave the march: the game took the move, but a party in a battle moves only once the battle ends, so the storm and the siege went on, and the agent's report rightly showed them ("The siege was lifted at 13:00, but the report shows the storm going on since 07:00, so he cannot leave until it ends"). And the bar should not have been met: two lords (strength 1,195) walking to a waypoint 8.6 ahead were counted "due in about 3 hours", 96.5 away by road.
- **`in_battle`** (a new name): the lord's party is in a battle now. besiege's withdrawal and storm bars wait while it is (`not in_battle`), and act the hour the battle ends; `storm_text` says "A storm of it is being fought now: nobody leaves it until it ends." The settings say so. A receipt for an order given to a lord in a battle says the order cannot move it until the battle ends.
- **An arrival at a holding** (`Forecast.ToHolding`) is along the game's planned way only when that way ends at the holding (within 5 of it or its gate); a party walking somewhere first (a waypoint, a camp) or standing still is reckoned by road (the game's table, else as the crow flies) at its pace over a day. Used by the relief lists and bars, the joiners of a battle and the report's arrival lines, which now also say what is left: "there in about 11 hours (...), 30.0 to go on its way", or "... by road at its pace, 60.0 to go by road". (Latif "going to Starkmoore, there in about 7.2 days" was right, 488 on his way: the agent read "19.6 from Baq", his nearest holding, as the distance to it.)
- **Joiners** of a storm or a battle are only lords walking there now by a way that ends there. Asta, "defending" Hollin Gate, stood 5.5 off through the whole storm and had been counted in within the hour.
- **A battle under way two rounds or more** is run ahead at the rates measured in it (`Forecast.Measured`): the battle is run by the rules from its start for the hours it has lasted, each side's blows are scaled by what it really knocked down against what the rules expected (0.2 to 5 times), and the rest is run with those blows (the other side's unseen morale taken as middling in both runs, so the measure carries it). At Hollin Gate the rules alone said the orcs would lose 16 men an hour and break in 3.4 days; 22 hours in, they had lost about 7 an hour, kept their morale of 100, and worn the defenders from 197 to 65 (their morale 20). Read in the game's code: the rules match it step for step (the strikes, the round every hour or half hour, a casualty when a random number below the troop's hit points falls under the blow's damage); what differs is the blows' weight between real troops, which the measure carries.
- **A raid with its defenders down** is not a battle to run ahead (it was "won in about an hour" every hour): the village is looted when its health runs out (`RaidEventComponent`: each hour the raiders take (the square root of their number + 5) / 900 of it, `DefaultRaidModel`; TAOM's raid model adds peoples' and lords' gifts), so the hourly copy keeps each village's health (`HitPoints`) and the line gives what is left, the fall an hour (measured between the last two hours, else by the rule) and when it is looted. A village's defenders are "led by the village", not "behind walls".
- **SIEGES AND BATTLES shows the realm's own sieges and battles and the ones in its sight** (the camp's leader or a party in the battle seen, the place in its eyes' reach), no longer every ally's: the fog check flagged 46 reports of allies' sieges, storms and raids far off. The fog check allows the places the realm's eyes reach.

### Arriving at a castle: its gate counts (since 0.12.1)

A lord sent close to a holding (`near`) goes to a reachable point by its gate, but the tools' `within` measured from the holding's centre, and the gate of a castle or town lies a median 2.9 (castles) and 3.3 (towns) from its centre, up to 7.1; villages have none. Górli stood 4.3 from Caradhras' gate (6.8 from its centre) and Oropherion's army 4.7 (7.8) for 15 and 14 days, each waiting in besiege's first step for "within 6"; Val at Sirannon stood 6.2 off. Nothing changed, so the agent was never told, and its plan still had them taking Caradhras. A siege began only when the game's point fell on the castle's side of the gate, which is likely why Nanduhirion's siege in run 20261024-2110-C2 "never began". A watch at a gate more than 3 from the centre (105 of the 221 castles and towns) never began either, and goto's "within 4" often fell short.
- **`within(place, r)`** to a holding is now the nearer of its centre and its gate (`World.Near`); `distance` is unchanged.
- **`near`** goes to a reachable point 2 to 5 from the gate (it was 3 to 6), inside "within 6" with room to spare. goto's close stop is "within 6" (it was 4).
- **A march that stops short** is called back once (`Runner.Stalled`): a lord whose move takes him to a place (`near`, `goto`, `enter`, `post`, `toward`) who has stood still (moved under 1) for 6 in-game hours, still more than 6 from the place (3 from a spot), out of battle, not inside a holding and not besieging: "Górli has not moved for 6 hours while marching on Caradhras, still 9.0 from Caradhras: he may be stuck on the map. The order is kept and he holds where he is; give him another order if he should go elsewhere." It is told once for each stand; moving on clears it.

### A host split into many parties counts as one (since 0.12.2)
Run 20261025-1609-C2, Mert: "I think its something about our defend order's calculation of an enemy host's strength - the enemy was dispersed in a couple of different parties". Alenon's army (strength 8,505) guarded Torech Emel (radius 30, `strike_below` 1, `shelter_at` 1.5). Its strike took the weakest lord near the castle, Narsha (1,751), one of a dozen lords marching to join Thrurg's army (about 17,000 together, 27 to 35 from the castle), and chased her 33 hours, 24 down to 2.2, until Alenon stood 32 from his castle, outside his own area. Only then did the forces within 30 of the castle reach 1.5 times his strength (12,922); he turned for home 26 from the walls and was caught 5 hours later by Bolga and 17 more parties (10,306 men against 3,587): 13 lords taken. The safety rule never fired, since it weighed each enemy party alone (Thrurg's army was then 3,913, each follower 700 to 1,800). The same rule let Thalion and Girda be caught by lords that came together in run 20261025-1524-C2. And the old 4-hour give-up never worked: the strike went back to patrol, which struck the same target again the same hour with a fresh count.
- **The host** (`World.HeadOf`, `World.HostOf`): a lord marching to join an army or a lord of his realm (the game's `EscortParty`) belongs to its head; his host is the head and every party in view marching to join it, within 20 of him (`World.HostReach`, about eight hours at an army's pace).
- **Safety counts the danger as a group** (`World.DangerAround`): every hostile lord, army and band of bandits in view within `danger_radius` of the lord, and the host of each of those lords (even of his own target, which itself stays spared), together against `caution` times his strength. Shelter and run take the whole group's strength, and the call-back says so: "Thalion is sheltering in Imnagath from Grisha and 1 more with it (strength 980 together against 600)". A shadow spares the army it shadows and its host unless they turn on it.
- **`threat_near(place, r)`** also counts the host of every enemy lord within r, wherever its parties stand.
- **New functions:** `company(x)`: the strength x would be fought with, its host and every enemy within `danger_radius` of it (`World.Company`); `easiest_enemy_near(at, r)`: the enemy within r whose company is weakest.
- **defend's strike** picks `easiest_enemy_near` and strikes only when its company is at most `strike_below` times its strength; it breaks off (with a call-back) when its company grows past 1.5 times that, when the chase would take the lord out of its area (`not within(at, radius)`), when the target leaves the area, or when it has not closed in by 1 for 6 hours; a target it broke off from or gave up on is left be for 6 hours. Out of a shelter it comes only once the area and half as far again beyond it are clear (`threat_near(at, radius * 1.5)`, the relief within `radius * 1.5`).
- **engage** gives up a chase that has not closed in by 1 for 8 hours, out of battle ("... no closer in 8 hours (1.2 away), so Hrakgar is as fast as Thalion or faster"), and its next order starts. **hunt** stops when its target's company reaches `bigger_ratio` times its strength.

- **`join_hours`** (default 8, 0 to 96), on every move and tool beside `caution` and `danger_radius`: the hours ahead an order's fights count the enemy lords and armies in view that could join them, each by its own pace as the crow flies (`World.Joiners`; a fight's place is no holding, so there is no road table to it). It replaces 0.12.2's fixed "host within 20":
  - caution: once anything hostile is within `danger_radius`, so a fight may start where the lord is, the threats within it and every enemy lord and army that could reach him within `join_hours` count together (`World.DangerAround`); his own target stays spared, but those who could join a fight with it count;
  - `company(x)` and `easiest_enemy_near(at, r)` (defend's strike, hunt): the target and every enemy lord and army that could reach where it stands within `join_hours`;
  - `threat_near(place, r)` in a tool's rules: the parties within r and every enemy lord and army that could reach the place within `join_hours`. Other uses (whether a refuge would hold) count only the parties within their radius, as before 0.12.2.

- **`engagement`** (war room, beside `can_catch` and `battle_odds`; `EngagementTool.cs`): your lords or armies (`ours`) against one enemy lord or army in view (`target`). Each of yours is routed to where the target is now (the game's way, at its pace); the fight begins when the first gets there (those within the hour with it) and the others join as they arrive. Every other enemy lord and army in view (the 30 nearest routed) gets the hour it could march there. The fight is run by the game's battle rules (`GameBattle`, the enemy's unseen morale low, middling and high) against the target alone, then with every enemy that would arrive while it is still going on; each is marked "in time to join it" or "too late: the fight is over by then". It ends with when the fight would end from now and "give that order join_hours N". A target on the march is flagged (the fight may come later and elsewhere; `can_catch` says whether it can be caught).

### Breaking a siege: when the defenders inside join (since 0.6)
Read in the 1.4.8 code (`SallyOutsCampaignBehavior`, `StartBattleAction`, `MapEvent.Initialize`, `Town.GetDefenderParties`):
- A relief force that attacks the besiegers starts a "siege outside" battle. All the besieging lords fight it together.
- **The garrison and the lords sheltering inside** (the realm's and its allies') sally out to join that battle only when they, plus the friendly forces near the siege camp (the relief among them), are **more than 1.5 times** the enemy forces near the camp. The game checks this when the battle starts and again every hour while it lasts. (Without a relief, the bar for a sally on its own is 2 times, checked every 4 hours.)
- **Militia never sally out.**
- So **relieve** and **defend** now weigh `relief_strength(at)`: the relief's own strength, plus the garrison's and the inside lords' (`inside_strength(at)`) when that rule holds, against the besiegers'. A lord sheltering inside is not counted twice. READY-MADE FACTS give the strength inside a besieged holding of the realm and the rule.
- **Ordering the army:** orders go to the leader, and the leader's moves and tools move the whole army. A party marching in an army cannot be ordered.
- **The game still breaks armies up:**
  - idle (holding, or waiting at a friendly place) for about two days;
  - cohesion run out;
  - more than half of its parties starving;
  - no war left.
- **Saves:** armies are ordinary game armies, so they are saved like any other. Only the hold on the leader is handed back around every save.

## The language

A tool is a JSON file:
- `settings`: each written [default, min, max, "what it is"];
- `init`: values remembered from the start;
- `start`: the first state;
- `states`: each has:
  - `about`: what the orders in force say it is doing;
  - `do`: the move made when the state is entered;
  - `when`: ordered rules (`if`, `go`, optional `poke` and `set`). The first rule that holds fires.
- A state with `end` finishes the tool: `free` hands the lord back to the game's AI, `hold` keeps the lord standing, and `fail` says the tool could not be done: the lord is set free and its next order does not start (since 0.5.1); `stop` (0.7.4) keeps the lord standing where it is and its next order does not start (besiege's withdrawal, once inside its refuge). Otherwise a `then` order starts instead, if there is one.

The defend tool, shortened:

```json
"patrol":  { "about": "patrolling around {at}", "do": "patrol at",
  "when": [ { "if": "besieged(at)", "go": "shelter", "poke": "A strong force besieges {at}..." },
            { "if": "enemy_strength_near(at, radius) >= strength * shelter_at", "go": "shelter", "poke": "..." },
            { "if": "weakest_enemy_near(at, radius) != none and strength_of(weakest_enemy_near(at, radius)) <= strength * strike_below",
              "go": "strike", "set": { "target": "weakest_enemy_near(at, radius)" } } ] },
"shelter": { "about": "sheltering in {refuge}", "do": "enter refuge",
  "when": [ { "if": "hours_in_state >= 3 and not besieged(at) and enemy_strength_near(at, radius) < strength * shelter_at * 0.8", "go": "patrol" } ] }
```

- **Moves:** goto, enter, near (close to a holding's gate), toward (where a party was last seen), leave, patrol, defend, engage, follow, join (follow an army's leader and join once it can be called), besiege, raid, recruit and hold; since 0.7.1, post (stand on a spot, or at a holding's gate, outside) and shadow (keep `keep` map units from a party, worked out again every hour); since 0.9.2, tour (the recruit order's place-by-place recruiting, above).
- **Expressions:** numbers, true, false and none; + - * /; == != < <= > >=; and, or, not.
  - A comparison with something unknown (none) is false, so no rule fires on a missing fact.
  - `{...}` in a call-back is filled in, with ids shown as names.
- **Names:**
  - the order's own: `me`, `at`, `target`, `lords`, and the settings;
  - about the lord: `strength` and `men` (the whole army's when the lord leads one), `ranks`, `wounded`, `food_days`, `pace` (since 0.11.6: map units an hour over a day, night counted, by the game's marching rules);
  - about time: `hours_in_state`, `hours_running`, `now` (the in-game hour, 0.7.1);
  - about armies and places: `in_army`, `leads_army`, `army_lords`, `army_coming`, `army_complete`, `army_joining` (lords holding a join order for this lord's army), `in_settlement`, `besieging`, and (0.7.5) `in_battle`.
- **Functions** (their names say what they do):
  - places and distance: `distance`, `hours_to`, `inside`, `within`, `fort`, `nearest_fort`, `nearest_supply`, `next_village`;
  - holdings: `besieged`, `siege_strength`, `inside_strength`, `relief_strength`, `besieger`, `raided`, `looted`, `ours`, `hostile`, `friendly`, `other_fort` (the nearest castle or town of the realm or an ally, not under siege, other than the one given);
  - enemies near a place: `enemies_near`, `enemy_strength_near`, `strongest_enemy_near`, `weakest_enemy_near`, `nearest_enemy`, `army_near` (enemy lords and armies in view), and `threat_near` (also bandits, and armies seen there in the last 6 hours), `enemy_threat_near` (the same without bandits); since 0.7.2 `relief_coming`, `relief_count` and `relief_of` (every enemy lord and army near a place or heading for it, together, in view or seen so in the last 6 hours: their strength, how many, and who, nearest first); since 0.7.4 `relief_within` (the part of it due within so many hours), `prep_hours` and `storm_text` (when the siege camp at a place may storm);
  - a party: `strength_of`, `men_of`, `in_view`, `seen_ago`, `gone`; since 0.7.1 `doing_of`, `toward_of` (the holding it heads for) and `where_of` (where it is, in words, with both distances), as the realm knows it (in view now, else as last seen);
  - the order's lords: `lords_arrived`, `lords_count`;
  - general: `min`, `max`, `pick`, `round`.
- **The checker** reads every tool before it runs: moves, functions, how many values each takes, names, states, ranges. Its problems are an alarm. The same checker will read the agent's own tools.

## What the agent reads besides the tools

- **WHAT CHANGED SINCE YOUR LAST ANSWER:** the reasons of every poke that came after the one it answered, and what became of its orders. The tools' call-backs are among them.
- **Distances (since 0.7.1; Mert, Oct 16: "distances should be given both as the crow flies and the actual travel distance"):** every distance in the report, the facts and the look-ups is given as the crow flies and then by road, "63.0 (260.0 by road)". The road figure is the game's own: its table of the real way between every two holdings' gates (`MapDistanceModel.GetDistance(Settlement, Settlement)`, copied at load into a `RoadBook`), and from a party through the holding whose gate serves the ground it stands on (`GetClosestEntranceToFace`, copied hourly as `PartyRow.Entrance`), as `DefaultMapDistanceModel.GetDistance(MobileParty, Settlement)` does (`Roads.To`, `Roads.Between`). "The nearest of your holdings" is now the nearest by road, the one a party could reach soonest. The poke rule's `sighting_distance` is by road, and the facts' and look-ups' travel hours too. The tools' own rules (radius, within) stay as the crow flies.
- **YOUR ORDERS IN FORCE:** each held lord's order, what it is doing right now (sheltering, buying food, waiting for an army), its settings that differ from the defaults, and its next order.
- **READY-MADE FACTS:** for up to four things the poke is about (a holding, an enemy), the realm's lords who can get there soonest, with strength, rough hours and whether they are free; and the enemies near it. Since 0.6 also:
  - **the muster** at the first holding the poke is about: how many lords, men and strength could be there within one, two and three days, as running totals, and how many of the first day's a leader there could call into an army at once;
  - for a besieged holding of the realm, the strength inside and the sally rule above;
  - since 0.7.4, under each enemy, a fight with it in the open by the game's battle rules: the realm's nearest lord alone and its three nearest together (above).
- **The report** (since 0.6) opens with **YOUR REALM IN SUM** (lords leading parties, their men and strength, armies, how many hold the agent's orders and how many are led by the game's own AI; castles and towns with garrisons, militia and sieges; villages looted or raided; lords held prisoner) and lists **YOUR CAPTURED LORDS**, grouped by where each is held (a holding, or the party holding them) and since when. They come from the game's own prisoner records, which the encyclopedia shows a player too, so the fog check allows their ids.
  - **YOUR HOLDINGS** (since 0.6.3): each castle and town with its wall level, its defenders' strength (garrison, militia and the lords inside) and the besiegers' strength it holds against, by the same assault test as the shelter rule (walls worth 2.4 / 2.8 / 3.2), with one line saying how the test works: above that, besiegers may storm at any hour, the sooner the stronger; below, they wait, but after four days of siege the bar falls hour by hour, to the defenders' bare strength by about the sixth day (`BesiegerCamp`, 1.4.8). Before, the agent saw garrison men only, and could not match a defence to an attack (Oct 16, Western Moria against Aragorn's 14,695).
  - **ENEMY REALMS, AS THEIR ENCYCLOPEDIA PAGES SHOW ANY RULER** (since 0.7.4): each enemy realm's lords and their parties' strength in all, beside the part in sight (above).
  - **IN VIEW** lists at most 40 parties, cut in this order since 0.6.3: allies and neutrals first, then bandits, then enemy lords; an enemy army is never cut. Before, it was cut by distance from the nearest holding, and on Oct 16 the 15 to 24 bands of bandits near the villages kept Aragorn's army of 5,532 men off the list for days, though the realm saw it. A sighting and the last-seen list now give an army's strength too.
  - **ENEMY ARMIES' TRAILS** (since 0.6.4; Mert, Oct 16: an army that turns aside to recruit, gather supplies or strike a weak enemy has not changed its main aim, and the agent should be able to see that): for each enemy army, where the realm saw it over the last two days, oldest first, with what it was doing and how far it was from the realm's nearest holding. A point is added each hour it is in view that its course changed, after every 10 map units of the same march, every 6 hours, or when it comes back into sight ("(out of sight 4 h)"); 12 points at most, and only trails of two points or more are shown. A trail ends when its army is seen broken up, or is gone. Only what the realm saw, so the fog rule holds. The enemy's look-up file carries the same trail, one point a line. A new doctrine principle (15, [DOCTRINE.md](DOCTRINE.md)) asks the agent to judge an army's aim by its course over days.
- **Look-ups** (`lookups\<id>.txt`), rewritten at each poke. The war room lets the agent read them:
  - one per holding: lords by march time, enemies near, enemy castles near; for a castle or town, the full muster (every lord by how soon they could be there, with running totals, * for those a leader there could call at once);
  - one per lord: men, strength, ranks, food, hours to each holding, enemies near;
  - one per captured lord: where and since when, and the lords who can reach that place soonest; `captured.txt` lists them all by place;
  - one per enemy the realm knows of: where it is or was last seen, its strength, the lords who can reach it soonest.

  Travel hours are rough: straight-line distance over the lord's speed. The calling range is checked the same way, so it is a little generous: the game measures along the way.
- **The council's briefing** (`council.txt`, since 0.6; every in-game hour until 0.12.0, since then when the war room asks as a council sits, from the newest hour, [WARROOM.md](WARROOM.md#the-council-of-war)): the whole realm at once for the war room's council of war ([WARROOM.md](WARROOM.md#the-council-of-war)): the purse (the ruler's clan's gold and influence, every other clan's influence), every lord with ranks, food, speed, what it is doing, whose judgment it follows and its clan's influence, the muster at each castle and town, and the captured lords.
- **Its memory** (since 0.5, [MEMORY.md](MEMORY.md)): the story so far, its own notes, and its chronicle, of which the report's last section shows the last two days. A tool's call-backs and endings go into the chronicle, so the story remembers which orders worked.

## The language grows (since 0.13)

What the agents' own tools needed and the language lacked ([PLAN-0.13.md](PLAN-0.13.md) A2; Mert's circuit is the test):
- **Lists and tables:** an order's `places` (a list of holdings, each of the kind the tool takes, checked against the game when the order is given, `Orders.PrepareTool`) and `lords` read as lists; `[a, b]` writes one; `{}` is an empty table, read as `visited[stop]` (none for a key never set) and set by a rule as `"visited[stop]": "now"`; `places[0]` is a list's first item. Each `{}` is a new table.
- **takes** names each thing a tool takes with its kind first and the writer's words after it (`"places": "friendly_holding: the towns to recruit at"`), shown in the library beside the kind.
- **Moves:** `recruit at X` (one stop, by the recruit order's rules, taking new volunteers as they come), `recruit_here`, `sell at X` (into a town or castle of the realm's or an ally's to sell loot; a lord already inside sells at once), `wait N` (N hours where he stands). A state with no move only decides: the lord keeps the move he has; a state with no move, no end and no rules is a dead end and is refused.
- **Facts:** `gold`, `clan_gold`, `loot_value`, `prisoners`, `ransom`, `wage_cap_reached`, `ranks_full`, `recruited_last` and `sold_last` (from the last stop and sale of this order, `RecruitTour.Fill`, `Purse.Fill`), `arrived` (inside or at a holding, within 6 for near, at a spot, a wait's hours passed; a patrol or a chase never arrives), `hour_of_day`, `day`.
- **Functions:** `count`, `first`, `last`, `item`, `contains`, `next` (round to the first), `nearest_of`, `next_due(places, table, hours)`, `hours_since`, `weakest_bandit_near`, `bandits_near`, `nearest_town` (the realm's or an ally's, not besieged), `nearest_town_of_realm`.
- **The checker** names each problem's place ("state recruit, rule 2 (\"...\"): next_due takes 3 values, given 2"), finds states no rule reaches and dead ends, and refuses a setting or a remembered value named like a function or a built-in.
- **A lord on his way to a recruit or sell stop** who stands still for 6 hours is called back as stuck, as on any other way (`Runner.Stalled`).

## The agents' own tools (since 0.13)

Mert, Oct 28: "If the agent wants, it can keep the tool it has written as a one-off order, or if it thinks the tool has reuse value, it can add it to its tool/order library so it is able to reuse it." Our 20 tools stay the agents' to order, and their whole definitions are readable ([PLAN-0.13.md](PLAN-0.13.md) Part A).
- **In an answer** (`tools`, read before its orders, `Channel.TakeTools`): `{"name", "keep", "about", "definition"}` (or the definition's parts at the top). Each is checked (the same `ToolDef.FromJson` as ours, with the agent's realm as its author); one that fails is refused with its problems, and so are the orders naming it. Without `keep` it is used once, for that answer's orders (`one_off`). With `keep` it goes into the realm's library; the same name again with a new definition makes the next version (orders already running keep theirs, each order carries its very `ToolDef`); the same definition again changes nothing; `"retire": true` takes it out. In shadow mode a tool is checked only. Each tool gets its own receipt line ("KEPT. circuit v1 is kept in your library ...").
- **Rules for a tool of the agent's** (A5): 12 states, 40 rules, 8 settings, 600 characters an expression; a name of 2 to 32 small letters, digits and underscores, never one of ours nor an order word (free, release, adjust, add, disband, wages, party_ai, then); no army tools; the safety rule always on (it gets `caution`, `danger_radius` and `join_hours` as ours do). At most 3 changes of state an hour (as ours). At most 6 call-backs an in-game day, the rest held and told in one when the day is out. Over 5 ms of the helper's time an hour for 3 hours running, it is stopped, its lord let go and the agent told (`Runner.Timed`).
- **The realm's library** (`Tools/OwnLibrary.cs`): the channel folder's `library\` (`index.json`, and `<name>.v<N>.json` for each version as written), read at every load, so it carries over between loads, campaigns and runs. Your realm has one, kept in its own agent's folder (switched on or not) and shared with the lieutenant (`OwnLibrary.SetHomes`). At most 30 kept tools. Each kept tool's record (runs by how they ended and by version, call-backs, hours, men, gold and strength over its runs, battles fought and won, lords taken and lost, holdings gained and lost, the test runs it was used in) is summed from its runs' outcomes (`Outcomes.End`). A run's setup lists each realm's kept tools as it begins.
- **What the agent reads:** `tools.txt` per channel gains YOUR OWN TOOLS after ours, each with what it takes, its settings and its record (records refreshed once a day, so the cached system prompt changes rarely); `tools\<name>.json` holds every tool's whole definition, ours and its own. Its instructions' "Writing your own tools" has the language in full and three examples (the circuit, a gate watch, an army's sortie), each checked by the smoke test.
- **Check a tool** (`check_tool`, served beside "ask the way", `ToolCheck.cs`): the game's own checker, linked into the war room (`Tools/Expr.cs`, `Tools/ToolDef.cs`, the library's JSON embedded, Newtonsoft 13.0.1), with each problem's place. With a lord named, the try: the war room writes `orders\toolcheck-request-<id>.json`, the bridge's helper answers `toolcheck-<id>.txt` on its urgent lane from the realm's newest hour (`ToolTry`, `Runner.Try`: the start state's move, each rule with whether it would fire and its names that are none, where the first that fires leads as the runner goes in an hour, what it remembers and calls back; moving no one, recording only the try), and the war room waits up to 10 s. `WarRoom toolcheck --write` is a real short call that writes, checks, fixes and answers with a tool.
- **The toolbook** (`toolbook.jsonl`) gains every tool written (`tool_written`, its whole definition), its check (`tool_check`, its problems), what became of it (`tool_kept`, `tool_changed`, `tool_unchanged`, `tool_one_off`, `tool_retired`, `tool_refused`, `tool_checked`) and each try (`tool_try`).

## Testing by hand

- `agents.tools` lists the library and says whether every tool passed the checker. `agents.tools defend` shows one tool.
- `agents.order <lord> <order> <place or lord> [setting=value] [lords=a,b]` gives the same orders by hand, always carried out. Examples:
  - `agents.order Throkmaw defend Caradhras radius=25`
  - `agents.order Krumbash gather Methedras lords=Naglur,Vughza`
  - `agents.order Gashnar adjust caution=0`
- `agents.orders` lists every held lord with its tool and its current move. `agents.release <lord>` or `agents.release all` hands lords back.
- In the logs (docs/LOGGING.md): `tool` events for every start, step, call-back, shelter and end; `army` events; `order` events for every move made.

## Limits we know

- **Rules are checked once an in-game hour,** as the game's own AI thinks. At fast speed that is about a second.
- **The storm rule acts only for a camp the order's lord leads** (the hook looks the rule up by the camp leader's party). A lord besieging inside an army someone else leads has its rule ignored; the leader's own AI decides.
- **When a tool attacks a lord, the game's move heads for that lord's true position,** as the game's own AI does. A lord out of view is searched for where the realm last saw it (toward), so the tools themselves never use what the realm cannot see.
- **Strength is the game's own estimate,** for a party, an army, a garrison or a siege camp. The game's sally check weighs a slightly different number (each party's strength for its side and the ground, times its aggressiveness) and counts every friendly and enemy party near the camp, so `relief_strength` is close to the game's judgment, not identical.
